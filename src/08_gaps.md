## SECTION 8 — Known Gaps and Open Challenges

**8a — The Horizon Problem and Structural Boundaries** `[STABLE]`

**8a.1** A complete epistemic treatise cannot consistently assert its own
    closure without violating the Principle of Rational Agnosticism (Section
    2e). The GMRTI explicitly recognizes its own horizon: it is a methodology
    for the localized, iterative reduction of semantic entropy, not an engine
    for the total elimination of misunderstanding.

**8a.2** *The Epistemic Asymmetry Boundary*: Communicating covenants operating
    under radically mismatched Domain Risk Coefficients (DRCs) cannot achieve
    stable comosí through single-pass transmission. When one party operates at
    DRC = 0.9 (catastrophic liability) and another at DRC = 0.1 (exploratory
    dialogue), low-cost verification produces an Anchor-Only Subgraph in the
    lower-risk covenant that remains invisible until operational failure. The
    Meta-DRC protocol (3e.1v) resolves the procedural standoff by defaulting to
    the higher DRC, but the structural burden of asymmetric verification
    remains an irreducible friction cost. `[STABLE]`

**8a.3** *The JIT-OM Ceiling and Undetected Divergence*: As formally declared in
    Section 5b.3, Just-In-Time Ontological Marking is strictly triggered by
    *detected* edge divergence. Where shared surface vocabulary creates a false
    comosí that persists across all standard test vectors, divergence remains
    latent. This limitation is architectural: no finite diagnostic sequence can
    guarantee the absence of un-encountered boundary conditions. The GMRTI's
    defense is Polyphonic Encoding and Path-Stripping Probing, but these reduce
    risk asymptotically rather than eliminating it. `[STABLE]`

**8b — Active Theoretical Contentions** `[PROVISIONAL]`

**8b.1 — Contention 1: Graph-Space Partitioning and Individuation in Semantic
Entropy** `[PROVISIONAL]`

The mathematical formulation of Semantic Entropy in Section 1a ($H_s(S) = \log_2
|\{G_R : G_R \text{ compatible with } S\}|$) defines entropy as a Hartley-style
log-count over compatible reconstructed graph topologies. The open contention is
that this formulation presupposes a rigorous individuation criterion for
distinct, non-isomorphic edge-configurations. Partitioning graph configuration-space
is inherently covenant-relative: determining what constitutes an operationally
distinct reconstructed graph requires an external partition, directly engaging
the metaagnostic boundary.
- *Status*: Open. Currently operationalized heuristically via the Behavioral
  Convergence Proxy (Path-Count Metric) terminating at the Operational Action
  Threshold (§0.4, §1a.6). Formalizing invariant graph-space discretization
  remains targeted for downstream verification.

**8b.2 — Contention 2: Mirror-Prior Synchronization in Adversarial Loops**
`[PROVISIONAL]`

The Refinement Cycle's LLM-Manifesto loop (Section 6b) relies on an adversarial
mirror to surface voids and edge divergences. The critical vulnerability is
*Mirror-Prior Synchronization*: when the adversarial model shares the training
priors or cultural covenant of the author, it will systematically overlook the
same axiomatic blind spots. Interpretive charity is structurally reduced, but
shared axiomatic distortion remains undetected.
- *Mitigation & Resolution*: Enacting the Temporal Stranger Protocol (Section
  6c) and cross-model adversarial audits with opposed prompt-covenants.

**8b.3 — Contention 3: Holistic Boundary Leakage in Encapsulation**
`[PROVISIONAL]`

Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation (Section 2h) treats repaired subgraphs as black
boxes with declared interface sets. However, natural language covenants exhibit
holistic interconnectedness: shifts in distant nodes can alter the inferential
context of interface terms, inducing silent semantic drift inside encapsulated
clusters without any interface violation having been triggered.
- *Mitigation & Resolution*: Coupling BTE with Dynamic Re-Anchoring (Section
  4b.3) and periodic Path-Stripping Probes across encapsulated boundaries.

**8c — Refinement Agenda** `[STABLE]`

**8c.1** The presence of open contentions does not invalidate the framework's
    operational utility. In accordance with the Methodology (METHODOLOGY.md),
    open challenges are formally marked as `[OPEN]` or `[PROVISIONAL]`,
    preventing them from masquerading as stable invariants while focusing
    adversarial audit attention precisely where theoretical work remains to be
    done.

**8c.2** Future iterations of the GMRTI progress by systematically resolving
    these contentions through the Ratified Execution Protocol, graduating
    provisional mitigations into stable architectural invariants only after
    adversarial survival.
