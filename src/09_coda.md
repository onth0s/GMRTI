## SECTION 9 — Coda

**9a — The Pebble and the Boulder** `[PLAIN]`

Sisyphus got a boulder — heavy, immovable, an eternal punishment. He pushes it
up the hill, it rolls back down, he pushes it again. The myth is about futility:
a task that never completes, a surface that never yields.

This document gives you a pebble instead.

Small enough to hold. Light enough to shape. But sharp enough to grind against
your shoe until you name the friction. The whole apparatus — the covenants, the
probing, the damping — is what you do with the pebble instead of pushing it up a
hill forever.

The pebble was never the problem. The un-named friction it was making against
the shoe was.
