# PEBBLE: The Guide to As-If Understanding

This guide explains how we communicate when we cannot perfectly define our
words. It is a plain-English summary of the meta-agnostic approach.

---

## 1. The Core Problem: Circular Words

If you look up a word in the dictionary, it defines it using other words. If
you look those up, they point to even more words. Eventually, you loop back to
where you started.

Because language is circular, there is no "ground floor" of absolute meaning.
We cannot make perfectly precise definitions, and we cannot guarantee that two
people mean the exact same thing when they use the same word.

---

## 2. The Solution: The "As-If" Agreement (Comosí)

Since we cannot have identical dictionaries in our heads, how do we still get
things done?
- **Making Noises Appropriately**: We use words that seem right for the
  situation.
- **The "As-If" (Comosí)**: We do not actually share a perfect match of
  meaning. Instead, our understandings are just close enough to work. We act
  *as if* we understand each other.

---

## 3. Why Agreements Break: Context Shifts

An "as-if" agreement has a limited range. It works perfectly in one setting
but fails when things change:
- *Example*: Two people at home easily agree on what is a "painting" vs. a
  "sculpture." But when they walk into a modern art gallery, their agreement
  shatters because their personal definitions no longer match the new context.

---

## 4. The Meta-Agnostic Posture

To communicate effectively, you must accept this uncertainty:
1. **Doubt Your Certainty**: Never assume your listener has the exact same map
   of a word as you do.
2. **Expect Drift**: Your own dictionary is not fixed. It changes constantly
   as you gain new experiences and live through time.
3. **Test the Fit**: Instead of assuming agreement, test it by seeing if your
   understanding holds up when you change the context or step outside your
   comfort zone.
