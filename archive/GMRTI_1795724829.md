## SECTION 0 — Preamble and Document Conventions

### 0.0 — Why This Exists
`[PLAIN]` Man is a creature of Science. Homo Sapiens, right? Epistemology didn't
begin with Newton describing the motion of rolling balls down slopes nor the
orbital trajectories of celestial objects that have been shaping human myth for
as long as we were capable of looking at the sky. No. Science began with burning
meat and learning it tasted better that way. Crushing rock and dirt and learning
that it could be heated and combined into better things. Planting seeds on the
ground and learning that that place now belonged to you and your peers.

And passing it along. And teaching it to your kids.

Man is a creature of Science. And there must be a Science of Understanding. It
has been long enough. It's time to pass it on to future generations.

### 0.1 Purpose of This Document
`[PLAIN]` When you try to explain an idea to someone, you can never be
completely sure they understood exactly what you meant — and they can't be sure
either. The GMRTI is a step-by-step method for finding where that gap is, naming
it, and closing it as tightly as the situation requires.

This document articulates the General Method for Refinement and Transmission of
Ideas (GMRTI) as a unified, operational framework for diagnosing and repairing
semantic divergence without sacrificing diagnostic precision.

Its core structural commitments are:

1. **Tiered Edge Taxonomy Economy:** Using a lightweight tiered approach
   (coarse cut for Covenant Declaration vs. finer cut via JIT-OM for
   misattribution repair) to prevent classification overhead from blocking
   transmission.
2. **Mirror-to-Generative Transition:** Formalizing the Divergence Report with
   an explicit artifact template to make the transition operational.
3. **Syntactic Edge-Forcing (§2g):** Requiring explicit primary relational
   operators in formalized declarations.
4. **Empirical Validation of Semantic Entropy (§1a, §2f):** Establishing an
   empirical protocol for the Semantic Entropy Metric by counting Polyphonic
   Encoding paths to reach Operational Parking.
5. **Meta-DRC Alignment (§3c.2, §3e.1v):** Codifying the protocol to handle
   divergence over Domain Risk Coefficient classification itself.
6. **Cognitive Load Distribution System (CLDS):** Governing GMRTI's authoring
   through strict YAML concept specifications (`specs/`), formal Status Markers
   (`METHODOLOGY.md`), and the Ratified Execution Protocol (`REFINEMENT.md`) to
   manage and prevent structural debt.

A standalone Lean 4 formal verification package for the mathematical claims
latent in this framework is maintained in `math/` and is explicitly outside the
scope of this treatise.

## SECTION 1 — The Problem the GMRTI Addresses

### 1a — The Fundamental Transmission Problem
`[PLAIN]` Every idea lives inside the person who had it. Moving it to someone
else is not copying — it is reconstruction. The receiver has to rebuild your
idea from the signals you send, using their own inner dictionary, which is never
quite the same as yours.

**1a.1** Every idea `[->structural]` exists, in its original working form, as a
    configuration of relations `[->structural]` within a semantic covenant.
    `[STABLE]`

**1a.2** Distinct experiential histories `[->causal]` produce non-identical
    semantic covenants; no two semantic covenants are identical, because each is
    the product of a unique sequence of experience, reflection, and linguistic
    inheritance accumulated over time. `[STABLE]`

**1a.3** Transmission of an idea from one covenant to another `[->inferential]`
    requires *reconstruction* (signal decoding `[->procedural]` edge mapping) by
    the receiving covenant: building an approximation of the original
    configuration using only the signals available at the boundary between
    covenants: language, gesture, symbol, artifact. `[STABLE]`


**1a.4** The signals available at that boundary are themselves ambiguous,
    because the symbols used to encode an idea within one covenant carry no
    guarantee of identical decoding within another. This is the metaagnostic
    condition applied to communication. `[STABLE]`

**1a.5** All apparent understanding is therefore a *comosí*: a functional
    overlap sufficient for practical coordination within a bounded domain, but
    not a guarantee of semantic identity. The comosí may hold perfectly within
    its established domain and collapse entirely when conditions change.
    `[STABLE]`

**1a.6 — Semantic Entropy as the Adversarial Target** `[STABLE]`

`[PLAIN]` The more ways a message can be misread, the higher its "confusion
score." The GMRTI's main job is to lower that score — not to zero, which is
impossible, but low enough for the task at hand.

The fundamental transmission problem can be restated precisely using the concept
of Semantic Entropy. A transmission event begins with the transmitter encoding a
semantic configuration — a specific topological region of their covenant — into
a surface signal. That signal reaches the receiver carrying high semantic
entropy: many non-identical edge-configurations in the receiving covenant remain
compatible with what was transmitted. The receiver selects one of these
configurations — typically without awareness that selection is occurring — and
treats it as the transmitted content.

We formalize Semantic Entropy $H_s(S)$ of a consistent surface signal $S$
relative to a receiving covenant and a **frozen, add-only operational probe
suite** as the log of the number of distinct functional equivalence classes
$[G_R]$ compatible with $S$ without contradiction ($|\{[G_R]\}| \ge 1$; an empty
set $|\{[G_R]\}| = 0$ denotes **Signal Contradiction / Unsatisfiability**, not
zero entropy; under the §8b.1 quotient, a class $[G_R]$ is compatible with $S$
iff at least one representative $G \in [G_R]$ satisfies $S$; dropping or
weakening probes mid-cycle is invalid, whereas expanding the suite can only
refine or increase $H_s(S)$): $$H_s(S) = \log_2 |\{[G_R] : [G_R] \text{ is
compatible with } S\}|$$ Because direct measurement of $H_s(S)$ is constrained
by metaagnosticism, we operationalize it via the **Behavioral Convergence Proxy
(Path-Count Metric)**: the number of independent Polyphonic Encoding paths that
must be successfully reconstructed and verified by the receiver before they
reach the Operational Action Threshold [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3,
§8b.1]. For a single pair of covenants $(C_T, C_R)$, this path count measures
pairwise reconstruction divergence (analogous to cross-entropy), not intrinsic
signal entropy directly: if each independent non-void path also exhibits
*filtering independence* over rival equivalence classes (contracting the
compatible class space by a factor $k > 1$, which topological route-independence
alone does not guarantee), the required path count scales as $O(H_s(S) / \log_2
k)$. Because correlated paths and Anchor-Only Subgraphs inflate the count while
lucky comosí deflates it, the metric is a **two-sided (confounded) estimator**
rather than a strict bound; it converges to a reliable estimator of $H_s(S)$
only when evaluated under minimal interpretive charity (Audit Mode, DRC → 1.0)
along combined Path-Stripping Probes (§2f.5iv) against the declared probe suite.
Thus, a high path count under those conditions indicates high initial semantic
entropy.

The GMRTI's central purpose is the controlled reduction of semantic entropy:
progressively constraining the space of consistent receiving-covenant
configurations until the residual divergence is either negligible for the
transmission's purposes, or explicitly locatable and typeable when it is not.
This is not entropy elimination — metaagnosticism establishes that as
unachievable — but entropy management: bringing the receiving covenant's
configuration into a neighborhood of the transmitting covenant's configuration
that is close enough for the work at hand.

This framing clarifies why natural language's high-entropy character is not
merely a stylistic inconvenience. Natural language is expressive precisely
because it is high-entropy: a word can mean many things to many covenants. This
is generative for poetry, metaphor, and creative connotation. It is structurally
dangerous for transmission of concepts whose downstream operational consequences
diverge sharply depending on which configuration the receiver selects. The GMRTI
does not propose to replace natural language's expressive entropy with formal
poverty. It proposes to selectively reduce entropy for the nodes and edges that
matter most in a given transmission context, leaving expressive richness intact
elsewhere. `[STABLE]`

### 1b — Why This Problem Is Not Merely Academic
`[PLAIN]` Misunderstanding is not just uncomfortable — it is dangerous at scale.
When people think they agree but actually don't, no amount of good intentions
will stop the plan from falling apart at the worst possible moment.

**1b.1** If transmission is always approximate and the degree of approximation
    is in principle unknowable without deliberate investigation, then:
    `[STABLE]`

- **1b.1i** Arguments that appear to be about substantive disagreements may in
    fact be about undetected transmission failures — the parties disagree about
    words, not things, without knowing it.
- **1b.1ii** Agreements that appear genuine may rest on comosí overlaps that
    will collapse under novel conditions, producing unexpected conflict
    precisely when coordination is most needed.
- **1b.1iii** Knowledge accumulated within one community may be effectively
    inaccessible to another not because it is hidden, but because the
    transmission medium — shared language, shared conceptual scaffolding — is
    insufficient to carry it.

**1b.2** These failure modes scale. At the individual level they produce
    miscommunication. At the community level they produce ideological
    fragmentation. At the civilizational level they produce the coordination
    failures that constitute the GMRTI's ultimate target domain. `[PROVISIONAL]`

**1b.3** The GMRTI's foundational thesis: a systematic method for locating and
    repairing transmission failures is a prerequisite for any General Method for
    Conflict Resolution, because without it, conflict resolution cannot
    distinguish genuine value disagreements from resolvable miscommunication.
    `[STABLE]`

### 1c — The Three Transmission Modes `[STABLE]`

`[PLAIN]` Misunderstanding happens across three scales: talking to your own
future self through old notes ([[Temporal Stranger]]), talking to another person
right now, and translating ideas between whole communities
([[Federated Graph]]). The GMRTI starts with notes to yourself first because it
is the safest place to practice before scaling up.

The GMRTI must address transmission across three structurally distinct modes
with a single unified algorithmic skeleton:

**1c.1 — Self-to-Future-Self Transmission** A thinker at time *t₁* attempts to
transmit an idea to their own future state at time *t₂*. The primary failure
mode is Temporal Drift Divergence: the author's covenant has changed between
encoding and decoding such that the originally intended edge-structure is no
longer recoverable from the encoded signal. The Temporal Stranger
operationalization (Section 6c) directly addresses this mode. This mode is
established as the canonical baseline sandbox for v0.9 under the practice of
**Self-Annotation**: applying GMRTI's Covenant Declaration, edge-typing, and
temporal-anchoring checks to one's own notes and artifacts as a single
practitioner.

**1c.2 — Self-to-Other Transmission** A thinker attempts to transmit an idea to
a contemporaneous other. This is the classical communication problem, understood
metaagnostically: as reconstruction across covenant difference rather than
transfer of identical content.

**1c.3 — Community-to-Community Transmission** A community's accumulated
knowledge, values, or frameworks is transmitted to another community with a
different collective covenant. This is the problem of ontological translation —
the target community may lack not just the words but the conceptual
infrastructure to receive the transmission. The Federated Graph model (Section
2d) addresses this mode's structural complexity [provisional dependency
boundary: §2d].

**1c.4 — Developmental Ordering** To prevent over-generalization and manage
cognitive overhead, the GMRTI mandates a strict developmental ordering:
Self-Annotation (Self-to-Future-Self) serves as the initial sandbox to generate
and test conceptual primitives; Community-to-Community (Constitutional Drafting)
[provisional dependency boundary: §2d] then formalizes these primitives; which
are subsequently simplified for real-time Self-to-Other dialogue (GMRTI-Mediated
Dialogue). The framework's operational complexity is scoped accordingly: v0.9
targets Self-Annotation only, where the cognitive overhead is borne by a single
practitioner with full access to their own covenant. Scaling to real-time
dialogue requires a simplified subset whose specification is deferred until the
Self-Annotation validation is complete.

## SECTION 2 — Foundational Architecture: The Topological Network Model

### 2a — The Edge-Primacy Structure of Meaning `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` A word has no meaning on its own — meaning comes entirely from how it
connects to other words and ideas. Change the connections, and you change what
the word means, even if the word itself stays the same.

**2a.1** A semantic covenant can be modeled as a directed graph in which edges
    represent the meaning-carrying relations between conceptual regions.

**2a.2** **Meaning `[->structural]` resides in relational edges, where
    edge-cluster density `[->identity]` constitutes the concept rather than an
    isolated atom.** A concept is not a self-subsistent semantic particle; it is
    a localized stability in a relational field. The label attached to that
    region
    is a handle, not a container of meaning in itself. `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`


**2a.3** This avoids the substantialist temptation: we are not positing a realm
    of Platonic concepts that language imperfectly points at. We are positing a
    network of relations, and "concepts" are simply the regions of that network
    that have achieved sufficient internal density to be worth labeling.
    `[STABLE]`

**2a.4 — The Edge-Grounding Resolution** `[STABLE]`

The topological model raises an immediate regress: if nodes are merely clusters
of edges, then edges must connect *something* — and that something appears to
reintroduce the node under a different name. This is resolved as follows:

- **2a.4i** Edges do not connect nodes in the sense of requiring pre-existing
    node-entities at their termini. Edges are *primitive relational events* from
    which all structure is built. What we call a node is an emergent density in
    the edge-field, not an input to it.
- **2a.4ii** The analogy is to field physics rather than particle physics: the
    field is primary, and particle-like stability emerges at regions of
    sufficient relational density.
- **2a.4iii** This means the edge-primacy claim is not viciously circular. The
    edges are not "between" pre-existing nodes; nodes are post-hoc labels for
    regions where edges have become sufficiently dense and stable to warrant
    naming. The naming is a convenience for transmission — it becomes a source
    of transmission failure when the label is mistaken for the thing itself.
- **2a.4iv — The Whirlpool in the River Allegory** `[ALLEGORY: EDGE-PRIMACY]`
    A whirlpool in a river is not an object that someone dropped into the water
    which then causes the water to rotate. It is simply what happens when
    rushing water forces meet, push against each other, and spin. The swirl (the
    noun, or node) is created entirely by the moving currents (the relations,
    or [[Edge Taxonomy|edges]] inside a
    [[Semantic Covenant|semantic covenant]]). Naming the whirlpool
    gives us a convenient word to point at, but treating it as a solid object
    independent of the river's moving forces is a mistake.

**2a.5 — Edge Taxonomy** `[STABLE]`

Not all edges are alike. The GMRTI therefore introduces a working partition of
relation-types, because transmission failure often occurs specifically when one
party silently treats an edge of one type as though it were another — a failure
mode termed Edge-Type Misattribution (see 2c.3).

This taxonomy is itself a conceptual carving subject to the framework's own
metaagnostic commitments. The boundaries between types are contested — the line
between inferential and causal edges, for instance, is a live dispute in
philosophy of science — and the partition's completeness is not guaranteed. It
is offered as a diagnostic instrument, not a closed ontological inventory.
Future iterations may collapse, subdivide, or reorder these types as the
framework's operational experience accumulates.

The current working partition introduces a **lightweight tiered approach** to
manage the classification overhead:

**Tier 1: The Primary Coarse Cut (For Covenant Declaration)** Used upfront in
Stage 1 to establish basic orientation without excessive cognitive load:
- **Inferential / Causal** — distinguishing logical consequence from worldly
  production.
- **Other** — deferred until required.

**Tier 2: The Finer Cut via JIT-OM (For Misattribution Repair)** Deployed
locally and retroactively only when an Edge-Type Misattribution is detected in
Stage 3:

- **Inferential edges** — what follows from what, and under which rules of
  consequence.
- **Causal edges** — what produces, enables, or constrains what in the world,
  independently of any reasoner's rules.
- **Procedural edges** — what sequence of operations constitutes a workable
  method for achieving an outcome.
- **Affective edges** — what is felt as linked, threatening, salient, or
  motivating; the emotional topology of a covenant.
- **Normative edges** — what ought to follow from what, and what counts as
  justification or obligation.
- **Identity edges** — what is treated as the same entity or concept for the
  purposes of reasoning or action.
- **Referential edges** — what expression points to what object, event, region,
  or other expression.
- **Structural edges** — non-reductive connective relations that organize a
  covenant's topology without collapsing into any of the diagnostic categories
  above; at the operational-role level (§2e.5ii), structural routing is
  composed from the three core primitives (`[->causal]`, `[->inferential]`,
  `[->identity]`).

**Taxonomy Switching Rule**: The GMRTI operates under the Tier 1 coarse cut by
default during initial Covenant Declaration (note: `[->identity]` is a core
primitive bootstrapped by CZP in §2e.5ii, whose fine-grained disambiguation from
`Structural` and `Referential` edges is deferred to Tier 2 unless flagged). If a
divergence is detected in Stage 2 and localized in Stage 3 as an Edge-Type
Misattribution, the JIT-OM protocol triggers the activation of the Tier 2 full
taxonomy locally for that subgraph, transitioning the classification from
'Other' or correcting the initial coarse cut to resolve the mismatch.

```table
TITLE: Two-Tier Edge Taxonomy
| Tier | Operator | Primary Function | Activation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (Coarse) | Inferential / Causal | Logical consequence vs. worldly production | Stage 1 (Covenant Declaration) |
| Tier 1 (Coarse) | Other | Deferred non-causal/non-inferential links | Stage 1 (Covenant Declaration) |
| Tier 2 (Refined) | Procedural / Normative / Affective | Method sequence, obligation, salience | Stage 3 (JIT-OM Repair) |
| Tier 2 (Refined) | Identity / Referential / Structural | Equivalence, designation, network tissue | Stage 3 (JIT-OM Repair) |
```


**2a.6** The illusion of understanding occurs when two covenants share a surface
    label — the same convenient handle — but have non-isomorphic edge-structures
    in the topological region that label points to. They are pointing at
    different parts of their respective fields with the same finger. `[STABLE]`

### 2b — Topological Depth Layers `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Some concepts are deeply buried — many steps away from anything you
can point at in the real world. The deeper a concept, the easier it is for two
people to think they share it when they actually don't.

Because concept-labels do not possess inherent substance, epistemic depth is
determined by topological dependency: how much of the rest of the network
depends on a given edge-cluster remaining stable.

- **Surface Subgraphs** — Edge-clusters that are highly context-dependent,
  easily renegotiated, and minimally load-bearing. Their alteration produces
  local disruption only. Transmission failures here are typically repairable
  through single-path clarification.
- **Structural Subgraphs** — Edge-clusters that route meaning between large
  regions of the semantic network. Revising these requires significant localized
  reconstruction and produces downstream effects on Surface Subgraphs that
  depend on them.
- **Axiomatic Edge-Clusters (Load-Bearing Regions)** — Edge-clusters with
  maximal structural centrality within the covenant: the rest of the covenant
  is organized around them. Their alteration requires the collapse and
  rebuilding of the covenant's identity. They are defined entirely by structural
  inescapability, not by any distinct ontological type. Crucially, they are
  rarely explicit — a covenant's axiomatic regions are usually invisible to its
  holder until extreme divergence pressure surfaces them.

**2b.1** The practical significance of depth is asymmetric: Surface failures are
    cheap to repair; Structural failures are costly; Axiomatic failures may be
    irreducible. A primary function of the GMRTI algorithm is to navigate
    efficiently from detected surface divergence inward to the layer where
    divergence actually originates. `[STABLE]`

**2b.2 — Detection via Dependency-Tracing Scan** `[PROVISIONAL]`

To locate Axiomatic Edge-Clusters without relying purely on subjective
introspection, the GMRTI prescribes the **Dependency-Tracing Scan**: an
algorithmic traversal calculating the downstream propagation impact of
hypothetical edge revisions within the **declared domain subgraph** (subject to
a domain-calibrated minimum subgraph size floor so trivial toy graphs do not
false-positive). Any declared edge-cluster whose alteration forces structural
reconfiguration in more than a declared fraction of dependent nodes in that
domain subgraph (provisionally calibrated to a 30% node threshold, subject to
domain-specific calibration) is classified as *Axiomatically Active*. Because
this scan operates strictly over *declared* edges, it cannot detect undeclared
axiomatic clusters; undeclared axioms must be surfaced via probe-driven
Path-Stripping Probing and PCP (§2f.5iv–v).


### 2c — Divergence Taxonomy `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` A map can go wrong in two main ways right now — it can be empty where
it should have something, or have the wrong thing — plus a third way when your
notes go stale over time. Knowing which kind of error you are dealing with tells
you how to fix it.

**2c.1** The GMRTI identifies three primary divergence types:

- **2c.1i — Anchor-Only Subgraph** — The
  transmitting covenant attempts to reference an edge-cluster for which the
  receiving covenant has a shared surface label (a word or artifact) but
  possesses zero internal relational depth attached to it. The receiver is not
  pointing at a void, but at a hollow shell. This is the deepest and most
  dangerous divergence type, requiring Polyphonic Encoding to construct internal
  topology behind the shared label.
- **2c.1ii — Edge Divergence** — Both covenants possess an edge-cluster
  associated with the same surface label, but the internal topology of those
  clusters conflicts. The covenants appear to share a concept but operate with
  it differently. This is the most common source of arguments that feel
  unresolvable while being, in principle, repairable.
- **2c.1iii — Temporal Drift Divergence** — Divergence arising not from covenant
  difference but from covenant change over time. The transmitting state of a
  covenant at *t₁* and the receiving state at *t₂* have drifted, producing
  mismatch between encoded and decoded edge-structure within what appears to be
  a single continuous covenant.
- **2c.1iv — The Empty Wrapper vs. Mismatched Currencies Allegory**
  `[ALLEGORY: DIVERGENCE-TYPES]`
  An *Anchor-Only Subgraph* is like an empty golden chocolate wrapper with no
  chocolate inside: a person can repeat the word "sovereignty", but has zero
  working operational connections attached to it. An
  *[[Divergence|Edge Divergence]]* is like two people both using the word
  "dollar", where one person means US Dollars and the other means Australian
  Australian Dollars: both hold real value and real rules, but their
  expectations clash the instant they try to make a trade.


**2c.2** These three types are not always cleanly separable in practice; real
    transmission failures often involve combinations. The taxonomy's value is
    diagnostic, not exhaustive. `[PROVISIONAL]`

```table
TITLE: Divergence Taxonomy & Diagnostic Signatures
| Divergence Type | Receiver State | Required Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor-Only Subgraph | Shared label with zero internal edges | Generative Scaffolding + [[Polyphonic Encoding]] |
| Edge Divergence | Shared label with conflicting topology | Local Alignment + [[Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation]] |
| Edge-Type Misattribution | Shared A-to-B link with mismatched type | Explicit Type Declaration via [[Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)\|JIT-OM]] |
| Temporal Drift Divergence | Covenant shift between t_1 and t_2 | Archival Reconstruction ([[Temporal Stranger]]) |
```

**2c.3 — Edge-Type Misattribution: A Subtype of Edge Divergence** `[STABLE]`

The introduction of the Edge Taxonomy (2a.5) reveals a subtype of Edge
Divergence that is easily under-diagnosed: **Edge-Type Misattribution**.

Edge-Type Misattribution occurs when both covenants possess the A-to-B relation
— neither party is missing the connection — but each assigns it to a different
edge-type. One party holds the connection as inferential (B follows from A by
logical consequence); the other holds it as causal (A produces B in the world).
One holds it as normative (A ought to lead to B); the other as procedural (A is
the method that produces B). The surface label is shared; the relation-type is
not.

This is diagnostically significant because it is invisible to standard
divergence detection. The parties each confirm that they connect A to B — so no
Anchor-Only Subgraph is flagged, and the apparent Edge Divergence may not
surface until downstream operational consequences reveal that each party was
reasoning from a structurally different kind of connection. An inferential edge
licenses deductive moves; a causal edge licenses predictive ones; a normative
edge licenses evaluative ones. Reasoning correctly from a misattributed
edge-type produces conclusions that appear internally valid but are
systematically incompatible with the other party's conclusions — and the source
of incompatibility is invisible without explicit edge-type declaration.

The repair strategy for Edge-Type Misattribution is therefore distinct from
standard Edge Divergence repair: rather than Local Alignment of topological
structure, it requires explicit declaration and negotiation of edge-type — which
is precisely the kind of JIT-OM operation the framework prescribes. Note the
Tier 1 detection ceiling: because Tier 1 lumps Normative and Procedural edges
into `Other`, misattribution between them surfaces only when downstream
operational probes diverge (§5b.3).

### 2d — Institutional Meta-Covenants: Federated Graphs `[PROVISIONAL]`
`[PLAIN]` A group or an organization doesn't have a single shared brain.
Instead, they share written rules, guidebooks, and definitions. To keep everyone
on the same page, those shared documents must be actively maintained and
updated.

**2d.1** Institutions and communities lack singular consciousness. They cannot
    hold a semantic covenant in the way an individual does. Their collective
    semantic structure is a *Federated Graph*: a distributed, externalized
    topology that no single constituent member holds in full.

**2d.2** Meaning is stabilized across a community via **Artifact Anchors** —
    written laws, canonical texts, standardized protocols. However, because
    artifacts are textual, they are themselves subject to Temporal Drift.
    Artifact Anchors can only function as immutable scaffolds when paired with
    an active **Maintenance Covenant** (a prescriptive interpretive body) whose
    explicit purpose is to continuously negotiate and counteract the artifact's
    drift using GMRTI principles. The anchor slows drift; the Maintenance
    Covenant manages the residual. `[PROVISIONAL]`

- **2d.2i — The Vault Kilogram and Measurement Scientists Allegory**
    `[ALLEGORY: ARTIFACT-ANCHORS]`
    A country agrees on what a "kilogram" means by keeping a polished cylinder
    of platinum in a sealed vault (the [[Artifact Anchor]]). But over the
    decades, dust gathers and tiny flecks of metal wear away. Without a
    dedicated team of scientists regularly cleaning, checking, and
    re-calibrating it (the Maintenance Covenant running
    [[Periodic Re-Mirroring Protocol|Periodic Re-Mirroring]]), every scale
    across
    across the nation will slowly drift apart without anyone realizing it.

**2d.3** The Federated Graph model has a critical implication: institutional
    transmission failures are not the same as individual transmission failures.
    An institution can transmit successfully even when no two members fully
    understand each other, provided Artifact Anchors are sufficiently robust.
    Conversely, an institution can fail to transmit even when all members
    believe they understand each other, if the Artifact Anchors have decayed
    faster than individual covenants have noticed. `[PROVISIONAL]`

**2d.4 — Countering Anchor Decay via Periodic Re-Mirroring Protocol**
`[PROVISIONAL]`

To combat Artifact Anchor decay before institutional failure occurs, the
Maintenance Covenant executes the **Periodic Re-Mirroring Protocol**: at
regularly scheduled epochs, the community's operating Federated Graph is audited
against the Artifact Anchor under the zero-charity Mirror posture (DRC → 1.0).
This produces a formal desynchronization *Delta Graph* that maps cumulative
semantic drift, triggering targeted maintenance repair before operational
coordination fractures.


### 2e — Rational Agnosticism as Operational Posture `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` When testing how well you explained something, pretend you are a
robot: use only the exact words on the page, and add nothing from your own
memory or friendly guesses. If the meaning still gets through, your explanation
is solid.

**2e.1** The GMRTI requires that any receiver engaged in deliberate
    transmission-testing treat themselves as a *Rationally Agnostic* entity: one
    who possesses only the edge-structures explicitly declared in the current
    transmission session, and who makes no appeal to unstated contextual grease,
    shared cultural assumptions, or inferred authorial intent.

- **2e.1i — The Literal Robot Stress-Test Allegory**
    `[ALLEGORY: RATIONAL-AGNOSTICISM]`
    When a friend says *"Hand me that thing by the door"*, you guess correctly
    because you know your friend and notice their eyes glance at the umbrella.
    That is natural human charity. Rational Agnosticism (acting as
    [[The Mirror]]) is testing your sentence with a literal-minded robot that
    knows only the words actually spoken: the robot halts and asks *"Which
    object? What door?"* Deliberately refusing to fill in the blanks with
    instructions before sending them to a stranger.


**2e.2** This is an operational posture, not a metaphysical claim. Real
    receivers are never truly rationally agnostic — they always bring existing
    covenants to bear. Rational Agnosticism is a *discipline of deliberate
    suspension*, not a description of a possible epistemic state.

**2e.3** The value of this posture is diagnostic: by temporarily suspending the
    interpretive charity that makes real communication feel smooth, it surfaces
    the hidden load-bearing work that shared contextual grease is doing — work
    that will fail when that grease is absent (across cultures, across time,
    under adversarial conditions). `[STABLE]`

**2e.4** Complete context-elimination — assuming that a transmission is only
    robust if it survives reconstruction by a receiver who lacks all contextual
    grease — is a useful approximation, not an architectural principle. Complete
    context-elimination is not a purified transmission environment; it is a
    pathological one. The GMRTI's goal is to make dependency on shared context
    *explicit and navigable*, not to eliminate it. `[STABLE]`

**2e.5 — Minimal Intelligibility Bootstrap** `[STABLE]`

To address the pathological mirror failure where low charity prevents any signal
decoding, we introduce the **Minimal Intelligibility Bootstrap (MIB)**. Rational
Agnosticism does not assume absolute zero knowledge. Every receiver presupposes
a baseline decoding substrate. The MIB stratifies this substrate into two
layers:

- **2e.5i — The Minimal Substrate** (framework-independent): The weakest shared
  assumption required for *any* structured communication to begin. It requires
  only that both agents can: (a) distinguish signals from non-signals; (b)
  detect structural similarity between relational configurations; (c)
  distinguish their own conceptual regions from one another (internal
  individuability). This substrate carries no GMRTI-specific content and makes
  no claim about edge-types, taxonomies, or relational grammar. `[STABLE]`

- **2e.5ii — The Primitive Covenant** (GMRTI-specific): The additional shared
  assumptions required for GMRTI-mediated communication specifically. This
  includes the three core primitive relational operators (`[->causal]`,
  `[->inferential]`, `[->identity]`) bootstrapped via CZP, the edge-primacy
  structural commitment (2a.2), and the Tier 1 coarse taxonomy
  (inferential/causal/other). Of the remaining five relational operators,
  `[->procedural]`, `[->referential]`, and `[->structural]` may be derived
  compositionally from the three core primitives, whereas `[->normative]` and
  `[->affective]` cannot be reduced to non-normative or non-experiential
  primitives and are negotiated locally via JIT-OM. The Primitive Covenant is
  itself a semantic framework subject to transmission error — its establishment
  between agents is a transmission problem that the MIB does not solve but CZP
  and JIT-OM address. `[STABLE]`

- **2e.5iii — The Covenant Zero Protocol (CZP)**: The procedure for
  bootstrapping the GMRTI-specific Primitive Covenant from the Minimal
  Substrate. CZP operates via ostensive reference and structural association
  games: (a) agents map causal relations by pairing physical signals with
  temporal co-occurrence (defining `[->causal]`); (b) agents map inferential
  relations by verifying pattern co-occurrence and logical consistency checks
  (defining `[->inferential]`); (c) agents map identity by establishing signal
  substitution (defining `[->identity]`). Once these core primitives are
  established, the coarse taxonomy is active and derived operators can be
  composed.

Calibrating the DRC to maximum (1.0) reduces interpretive charity to the Minimal
Substrate baseline. GMRTI-mediated repair additionally requires the Primitive
Covenant, whose sharing between agents is a precondition acknowledged as
non-trivial, not a background assumption that can be silently presupposed.

### 2f — Polyphonic Encoding `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` When something is really important, give more than one way to reach
the idea — like offering a definition, a picture, and a real-world example. If
one route is blocked, the others still carry the meaning.

**2f.1** *Polyphonic Encoding* is the deliberate provision of multiple
    structurally independent edge-paths to the same semantic destination within
    a single transmission act. It is the GMRTI's primary proactive transmission
    fidelity strategy.

**2f.2** Redundancy in transmission — the everyday name for Polyphonic Encoding
    — is not a stylistic failure or a sign of imprecision. It is an
    epistemically necessary response to the metaagnostic condition: since no
    single edge-path can be guaranteed to survive reconstruction within an
    unknown receiving covenant, encoding the same semantic destination via
    multiple independent routes increases the probability that at least one path
    arrives intact. `[STABLE]`

**2f.3** Polyphonic Encoding is structurally present in virtually every domain
    of effective communication, often without explicit recognition: `[STABLE]`

- **2f.3i** *Internet packet protocols*: the same data is routed through
    multiple physical paths simultaneously; if one fails, another carries the
    payload to its destination.
- **2f.3ii** *Pedagogy*: a teacher states the definition, provides a concrete
    example (`[ALLEGORY]`), offers an analogy, then asks a question that forces
    the student to produce the concept themselves. Each is a structurally
    independent route to the same conceptual destination.
- **2f.3iii** *Legal drafting*: a law defines a term, then provides an
    exhaustive list of cases it covers, then specifies cases it explicitly
    excludes — three independent routes to the same operational boundary.
- **2f.3iv** *This document*: key concepts are stated abstractly in Appendix G
    (Glossary), instantiated concretely via allegories where applicable (e.g.,
    §1a.5i, §1a.5ii, §7d.4), and contextualized within examples. Each
    independent instantiation (`[ALLEGORY]`, `[PLAIN]`, formal definition)
    serves as a distinct route to the same destination.

**2f.4** The relationship between Polyphonic Encoding and transmission depth
    layers is asymmetric and critical: `[STABLE]`

- **2f.4i** Surface Subgraph transmission failures are typically repairable with
    single-path clarification; Polyphonic Encoding is useful but not mandatory
    here.
- **2f.4ii** Structural Subgraph transmission failures benefit significantly
    from Polyphonic Encoding, as multiple routes reduce the probability of total
    reconstruction failure.
- **2f.4iii** **Axiomatic Edge-Cluster transmission requires mandatory
    Polyphonic Encoding.** Axiomatic claims are precisely those that
    transmitters are least likely to encode redundantly — because they are so
    foundational that the transmitter may not recognize them as claims at all,
    treating them instead as obvious background. This is the most dangerous
    single gap in spontaneous communication practice, and the GMRTI explicitly
    prescribes redundancy at this layer as non-optional. `[STABLE]`

**2f.5 — Redundancy-Masking: The Failure Mode of Polyphonic Encoding**
`[STABLE]`

**2f.5i** Polyphonic Encoding is not purely beneficial. Its critical failure
    mode is *Redundancy-Masking*: the phenomenon in which successful multi-path
    transmission conceals a local topological void that would produce failure
    under single-path conditions.

**2f.5ii** When an Anchor-Only Subgraph exists in a receiving covenant, a
    sufficiently rich Polyphonic Encoding may route around it entirely —
    arriving at the correct semantic destination via intact paths while leaving
    the void undetected. The transmission feels successful. The comosí holds.
    But the deficit remains, and when conditions change such that the redundant
    paths are unavailable (different context, different interlocutor, different
    domain), the void will produce catastrophic failure precisely when it is
    least expected.

**2f.5iii** Redundancy-Masking is therefore a source of *false comosí* —
    apparent semantic sufficiency sustained by backup routes that will not
    always be present (demonstrated allegorically by the shift from the domestic
    living room to the contemporary art gallery in §1a.5ii). The GMRTI must
    account for this: Polyphonic Encoding should be paired with explicit
    divergence-checking to ensure that successful transmission has not merely
    routed around a void rather than filled it. `[STABLE]`


**2f.5iv — The Detection of Redundancy-Masking via Path-Stripping Probing**
`[STABLE]`

To resolve the threat of a false comosí sustained by redundancy-masking, the
GMRTI introduces the **Path-Stripping Probing** protocol. The receiver
temporarily disables all but one polyphonic path, forcing the system to
reconstruct using only that single path. If reconstruction fails or drops below
the Operational Action Threshold [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3], a
hidden void is localized. This bounds the risk of redundancy-masking,
transforming it from a structural limit into a testable boundary.

**Polyphonic Node Detection**: PSP produces false positives on legitimately
polysemous/disjunctive hub nodes — nodes where a single lexical label routes
structurally independent, context-conditional meanings. To break the diagnostic
circularity without prematurely requiring Tier 2 edge-typing, the GMRTI defines
a two-tiered disambiguation pre-check (never automatic relaxation, since
standard polyphonic nodes also exhibit high in- and out-degree):
- **Tier 1 Structural Pre-screen**: In the default state, any node with
  in-degree ≥ 2 and out-degree ≥ 2 (regardless of edge-types) is flagged for a
  **Disambiguation Pre-Check**: test whether the incoming paths license the
  *same* downstream inferences across declared probes (convergent polyphonic
  node) or *context-disjoint* inferences (candidate disjunctive/polysemous hub).
- **Tier 2 Refined Pre-screen**: Where Tier 2 JIT-OM is already active,
  flagging requires two or more incoming edges of distinct types and two or more
  outgoing edges of distinct types, followed by the same inference-disjointness
  check.

PSP applied to confirmed disjunctive/polysemous hub nodes uses a strengthened
threshold: *all* paths must fail before a void is declared, rather than any
single path. On convergent polyphonically encoded nodes (destination-isomorphic
paths to a single meaning per §2f.6), PSP tests each path $p_i$ in isolation
with paths $p_{\neq i}$ stripped: if any single path $p_i$ drops below the
Operational Action Threshold [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3] when the
others are stripped, a local topological void on $p_i$ is confirmed.

CHECK{"Inference Check — Same inferences across probes?"}
CONV["Convergent Polyphonic Node — Test each path p_i in isolation"]
HUB["Disjunctive / Polysemous Hub — Void only if ALL paths fail"]
NODE --> CHECK
CHECK -->|Yes| CONV
CHECK -->|No| HUB
```

**2f.5v — The Latent Comosí Ceiling Resolution** `[STABLE]`

- **Null-Effect Equivalence Axiom**: If a latent topological divergence between
  two covenants has no behavioral consequence across all declared boundary
  conditions of the active domain, it is treated as operationally null under
  comosí [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3]. This null-effect status is
  strictly provisional and is invalidated whenever boundary conditions expand;
  it carries zero operational risk only within the currently declared domain.
- **Proactive Chaotic Probing (PCP)**: To detect latent divergences that *would*
  produce future failures, the GMRTI defines the PCP protocol. PCP proactively
  runs Path-Stripping Probing on random samples of polyphonically encoded
  nodes and candidate disjunctive hubs
  during scheduled maintenance intervals or dedicated audit states (high DRC),
  simulating path failure to force latent differences to surface before they
  trigger real-time faults.

- **2f.5vi — The Four-Engine Airplane Ground Test Allegory**
  `[ALLEGORY: PATH-STRIPPING]`
  A four-engine airplane can fly smoothly even if Engine #3 has a broken fuel
  pipe, because the other three engines supply enough lift to hide the problem
  during cruising flight
  ([[Redundancy-Masking]]).
  [[Path-Stripping Probing]] is the ground
  stress test where mechanics shut off all other engines so each engine is
  tested by itself. When Engine #3 is tested all by itself, it immediately
  sputters and stalls, revealing the broken pipe before the plane takes off into
  a storm.

**2f.6 — Core Path Minimization** `[STABLE]`

To prevent polyphonic complexity from expanding the graph's surface area and
introducing semantic drift, the GMRTI mandates **Core Path Minimization**:
redundant encoding paths must maintain *destination-isomorphism* — terminating
at the identical semantic destination and licensing the same downstream
operational inferences — while preserving *route-independence* across disjoint
intermediate topological structures. Redundant paths must refrain from
introducing extraneous unanchored concepts or unrelated semantic domains beyond
what is strictly necessary to construct the alternative route.



### 2g — Syntactic Edge-Forcing `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` If you force yourself to name the exact *kind* of relationship between
two ideas (like "A causes B" versus "A is just another word for B"), you avoid
misunderstandings before they can even start.

**2g.1** Polyphonic Encoding and JIT-OM address semantic entropy by respectively
    multiplying paths and marking ontological types at sites of detected
    failure. Both operate *around* the surface expression: one adds routes, the
    other adds labels. A third mechanism operates *within* the surface
    expression itself: Syntactic Edge-Forcing. `[STABLE]`

**2g.2** Syntactic Edge-Forcing occurs when the grammatical or compositional
    structure of an expression requires the receiver to traverse a specific
    relational path through semantic space as a constitutive condition of
    decoding the expression. The edge traversal is not optional — it is
    load-bearing in the very act of comprehension. `[STABLE]`

**2g.3** The clearest natural examples appear in oligosynthetic and
    root-compounding formal languages. When a concept is built compositionally
    from primitive roots — rather than assigned an arbitrary label — the
    receiver cannot understand the compound without activating the relational
    edge between its components. The grammar forces a specific traversal of the
    semantic network. This is structurally different from a natural language
    word, which assigns an arbitrary label to a concept without encoding the
    relational path by which the concept was reached. `[STABLE]`

**2g.4 — The Concatenation Insight** `[STABLE]`

In a compositional expression of the form `A + B`, the `+` operator is not
merely additive labeling. It is, at the structural level, a mandatory edge
declaration. The receiver, in order to decode `A + B`, must traverse the
relational edge between A and B. This traversal is enforced by the expression's
syntax; it cannot be bypassed.

We prescribe a formal set of canonical relational operators (the 3 CZP
primitives plus 5 derived/JIT-OM operators):
- `[->inferential]` (logical consequence)
- `[->causal]` (physical/worldly production)
- `[->procedural]` (methodological sequence)
- `[->normative]` (justification/obligation)
- `[->identity]` (semantic equivalence)
- `[->referential]` (object designation)
- `[->affective]` (salience/motivation)
- `[->structural]` (topological link)

A compositional expression that specifies not only *that* A connects to B but
*what kind* of connection it is — e.g., `A [->causal] B` versus `A
[->inferential] B` — achieves a stronger entropy reduction by preemptively
preventing Edge-Type Misattribution (2c.3). As an operational prototype, we
mandate that key stable claims in this treatise be annotated with Tier 2
edge-forcing operators as exemplars (see 2g.7).

- **2g.4i — The Jigsaw Puzzle Joint Allegory** `[ALLEGORY: EDGE-FORCING]`
  If you lay two flat, square wooden blocks side-by-side, someone could stack
  them, push them apart, or glue them backwards.
  [[Syntactic Edge-Forcing]] is cutting a specific tongue-and-groove puzzle
  joint into the wood: the blocks can only slide together in one exact
  alignment. The shape of the joint itself forces the connection, preventing
  [[Edge-Type Misattribution]].

**2g.5 — The Apodictic Comosí: Mathematical Language as Model** `[STABLE]`

The strongest known instance of Syntactic Edge-Forcing is mathematical notation.
The epsilon-delta definition of a limit does not merely label a concept — it
forces the reader to activate specific relational edges: universal
quantification scoping over existential quantification, implication
directionality, the metric structure of absolute value. One cannot read the
expression without traversing these edges; they are constitutive of the
expression's decodability.

The result is what the GMRTI terms an *apodictic comosí*: a functional overlap
between covenants so tightly constrained by syntactic structure that the
residual semantic entropy — while not literally zero — is negligible *relative
to natural language* within the domain defined by shared mathematical training.
(It is not negligible absolutely: convention disputes — open vs. closed
intervals, ring-with-unity vs. ring-without — demonstrate that even formal
notation retains residual entropy.) The definition of a limit can be stated in a
single formal sentence and received with near-univocal fidelity by any reader
who shares that training, without iterative repair, without Polyphonic Encoding,
without JIT-OM. The syntax has done the entropy-reduction work in advance.

**2g.6 — Scope and Limits of Syntactic Edge-Forcing** `[STABLE]`

Syntactic Edge-Forcing is not universally applicable. Its effectiveness is
bounded by two structural conditions:

- **2g.6i** *Shared primitive vocabulary*: Edge-forcing through composition only
    reduces entropy if the primitive components are themselves shared between
    covenants. If the primitives are themselves high-entropy — if their own
    edge-structures diverge significantly between covenants — compositional
    forcing propagates and amplifies rather than reduces entropy. This is
    precisely why mathematical edge-forcing works within communities sharing
    mathematical training and fails across communities that do not.
- **2g.6ii** *Concept-type limitations*: Syntactic Edge-Forcing is most powerful
    for concepts whose primary relational structure is logical or structural. It
    is weakest for qualia, normative concepts, and social constructs, where the
    most consequential edges are not logical dependencies but experiential
    associations or axiomatic commitments that no surface syntax can compel.
    Forcing the edge between "pain" and "undesirability" syntactically does not
    transmit the phenomenological content of pain; it only forces the logical
    label.

**2g.7** The GMRTI's prescription: Syntactic Edge-Forcing should be deployed
    where concept-type and shared primitive vocabulary permit it, as a
    complement to Polyphonic Encoding and JIT-OM — not as a replacement for
    either. Its deployment should be calibrated to the concept-type taxonomy of
    Section 5a. As Tier 2 Syntactic Edge-Forcing exemplars (distinct from Stage
    1
    Tier 1 coarse declarations), key stable claims in this treatise are formally
    edge-forced in situ:
- **§1a.1 (In Situ):** `Every idea [->structural] exists... as a configuration
  of
  relations [->structural] within a semantic covenant.`
- **§1a.2 (In Situ):** `Distinct experiential histories [->causal] produce
  non-identical semantic covenants...`
- **§1a.3 (In Situ):** `Transmission of an idea... [->inferential] requires
  *reconstruction* (signal decoding [->procedural] edge mapping)...`
- **§2a.2 (In Situ):** `Meaning [->structural] resides in relational edges,
  where
  edge-cluster density [->identity] constitutes the concept...`


### 2h — Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` In an interconnected web of ideas, fixing one broken piece can
accidentally shake other parts of the map. Encapsulation means drawing a clear
boundary around your fix so the ripples don't spread.

**2h.1** Under semantic holism, modifying an edge during localized repair
    propagates changes throughout the entire network, threatening the stability
    of unrelated concepts. To *attenuate* this propagation, the GMRTI introduces
    **Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation**.

**2h.2** Localized repairs must be enclosed in a boundary-covenant wrapper. This
    treats the modified subgraph as a single black-box node with a strictly
    declared interface (input/output edges). The wrapper attenuates edge
    propagation, confining the *direct* effects of meaning-shifts to the
    encapsulated region and reducing — though not eliminating — their
    destabilizing impact on the surrounding covenant graph.

- **2h.2i — The Submarine Watertight Bulkhead Allegory**
    `[ALLEGORY: ENCAPSULATION]`
    If a pipe bursts in one compartment of a submarine, the crew does not let
    seawater flood the entire vessel. They immediately lock shut the heavy
    watertight doors on that specific room (the
    [[Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation]]
    wrapper). They repair the broken valve inside the sealed room, ensuring only
    safe, controlled cables pass through the bulkhead to the rest of the ship.


**2h.3** BTE provides *practical* isolation, not *theoretical* isolation. Under
    edge-primacy (2a.2), the meaning of interface edges is itself a function of
    the surrounding graph. Changes outside the boundary that alter the
    interpretive context of interface edges can therefore propagate *indirectly*
    into the wrapped subgraph. BTE's guarantee is that no *new edges* cross the
    boundary except through declared interfaces — it does not guarantee that the
    *meaning* of those interfaces is immune to external context change. This
    residual holistic leakage is a known structural limitation, bounded in
    practice by keeping interface edge sets small and explicitly typed.
    `[STABLE]`

## SECTION 3 — The GMRTI Algorithm

### 3a — Overview and Termination Conditions `[STABLE]`

`[PLAIN]` The GMRTI works as a clear diagnostic cycle: map out the starting
concepts, test if the receiver can rebuild the map without guessing, locate any
broken links, and repair them. You stop when understanding is solid enough for
practical work.

**3a.1** The GMRTI algorithm is a recursive diagnostic-and-reconstruction cycle.
    It terminates when one of three conditions is met:

- **3a.1i** *Operational Parking*: the transmission has achieved a comosí of
    sufficient depth for the practical purposes at hand — operationally defined
    by the Operational Action Threshold [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3]
    as producing functionally congruent downstream actions or operational
    definitions within the specific bounded domain.
- **3a.1ii** *Epistemic Termination (Irreducibility Declaration)*: the
    divergence
    has been localized to the Axiomatic layer and confirmed as irreducible —
    meaning the parties genuinely differ in foundational commitments rather than
    in their understanding of each other (§3f).
- **3a.1iii** *Cost-Bounded Termination*: further refinement would exceed the
    DRC-adjusted benefit threshold. In low-risk domains, a weaker comosí may be
    rationally sufficient even when deeper repair remains theoretically
    possible. This condition prevents the framework from becoming
    self-consuming: a method that cannot say "enough for now" is
    indistinguishable from a method that cannot act. `[STABLE]`

**3a.2** Distinguishing 3a.1i from 3a.1ii is one of the framework's most
    demanding practical challenges. Distinguishing either from 3a.1iii is
    equally important: some transmissions are not unresolved because resolution
    is impossible, but because it is not worth the remaining cost of further
    repair. Neither error — premature Operational Parking leaving latent
    divergence, premature Irreducibility Declaration foreclosing available
    repair, or misidentifying a Cost-Bounded case as one of the other two — is
    trivially avoidable. `[STABLE]`

**3a.3** The GMRTI is not a compiler. A compiler operates on formally specified
    inputs with deterministic rules; the GMRTI's entire raison d'être is that
    such formal specification is unavailable. The compiler metaphor is useful
    precisely where it highlights the iterative, error-surfacing character of
    the process — and must be abandoned where it implies deterministic precision
    the framework structurally cannot provide. `[STABLE]`

Calibrating DRC or navigating termination conditions requires balancing risk
against cost:

**3a.4 — Tiered DRC and Opportunistic Agnosticism** `[STABLE]`

To balance the cognitive overhead of Rationally Agnostic reconstruction against
the necessity of creative generation, GMRTI defines three operational modes
governed by the Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC), defined on the bounded scale
[0.0, 1.0]:
- **Generative Mode (Drafting)**: DRC is calibrated to minimum (DRC → 0.0),
  enabling maximum interpretive charity. The receiver reads the transmission
  with full natural language assumptions. Verification overhead is zero.
- **Refinement Mode**: DRC is set to moderate levels (DRC ≈ 0.5). Basic path
  redundancy is checked, and core conceptual definitions are marked with Tier 1
  coarse taxonomy.
- **Audit Mode**: DRC is calibrated to maximum (DRC → 1.0). Interpretive
  charity is suspended. The receiver operates under the Mirror posture, using
  only the Minimal Substrate to perform Generative Reconstruction, forcing
  latent differences to surface for repair.

The framework deliberately leaves the assignment of numerical DRC values to
specific domains un-prescribed. GMRTI is a structural diagnostic instrument for
detecting and repairing semantic divergence, not an arbiter of moral or
political stakes. Deciding whether a domain carries "high" or "low" risk
requires normative value judgments that belong exclusively to the participating
covenants, not to the framework itself.

|---|---|---|---|
| Generative Mode | DRC -> 0.0 | Maximum | Suspended (drafting) |
| Refinement Mode | DRC ~ 0.5 | Moderate | Tier 1 coarse cut + redundancy check |
| Audit Mode | DRC -> 1.0 | Minimal Substrate only | Strict Mirror enforced throughout |
```

### 3b — Stage 1: Covenant Declaration `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Stage 1 is laying your cards on the table. You list the main ideas you
are using and clearly show how they connect to one another, so the other person
can see what you are building on.

**3b.1** The transmitting party makes explicit the semantic starting conditions
    of their communication. This involves:

- **3b.1i** Identifying the key topological regions in their covenant that the
    transmission will rely upon.
- **3b.1ii** Making explicit, where possible, the edges connecting those regions
    — not just labeling them but describing how they connect to each other and
    to the rest of the covenant.
- **3b.1iii** Flagging any regions the transmitting party suspects may be
    Anchor-Only Subgraphs in the receiving covenant.
- **3b.1iv** Applying Polyphonic Encoding to any claim suspected of operating at
    the Structural or Axiomatic layer — providing multiple independent routes to
    the same semantic destination.
- **3b.1v** `[STABLE]` Stage 1 applies only the Tier 1 Coarse Cut
  (Inferential / Causal / Other) in Refinement and Audit modes (moderate-to-high
  DRC) for initial Covenant Declaration orientation; in Generative Mode (low
  DRC), taxonomy tagging at declaration is optional. Tier 2 8-type granularity
  is strictly deferred to Stage 3 JIT-OM divergence resolution.


**3b.2** Covenant Declaration is not a demand for agreement. It is a demand for
    *visibility* — bringing the transmitting covenant's architecture to a level
    of explicitness where divergence can be detected rather than assumed away.
    `[STABLE]`

**3b.3** A complete Covenant Declaration is an ideal; in practice, only partial
    declarations are achievable, because any declaration is itself made in
    language, which inherits the metaagnostic condition. The goal is maximum
    achievable explicitness within this constraint. `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`

### 3c — Stage 2: Generative Reconstruction and Interpretive Charity Calibration `[STABLE]`

`[PLAIN]` Stage 2 has two beats: first, read strictly what is written without
guessing; second, use what you already know to fill in the gaps and write down
every spot where you had to guess.

**3c.1** Stage 2 is temporally divided to resolve the tension between pure
    reception and active interpretation:
- **3c.1i (Mirror Mapping Phase):** The receiving party operates strictly as a
  Rationally Agnostic Mirror, suspending interpretive charity to passively map
  the transmitting party's declared edges exactly as stated, identifying
  structural gaps.
- **3c.1ii (Generative Bridging Phase):** Interpretive charity is calibrated to
  the active DRC and re-engaged. The receiver uses their own covenant's
  topology to build active bridges and scaffolds across the gaps identified
  during the Mirror Mapping Phase. `[STABLE]`

**3c.2 — The Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)** `[STABLE]`

The required precision of reconstruction scales inversely with interpretive
charity, governed by the DRC:

- **High-DRC Domains** (aviation, surgery, law, formal logic): interpretive
  charity approaches zero. Any minor Edge Divergence triggers immediate
  localization and repair before transmission proceeds. The Mirror posture is
  sustained throughout.
- **Low-DRC Domains** (casual dialogue, early-stage creative brainstorming,
  arts): interpretive charity is maximized. Minor divergences are treated as
  noise, provided the Operational Action Threshold [provisional dependency
  boundary: §3c.3] is met. The Mirror posture is adopted only when a divergence
  becomes operationally significant.

**Meta-DRC Divergence**: A meta-divergence arises when the communicating parties
disagree on the DRC classification itself (e.g., Party A demands High-DRC
precision while Party B operates under Low-DRC charity). This is flagged as a
Meta-DRC Divergence during Stage 2. When detected, the standard transmission is
paused, and the algorithm redirects to Stage 3 Localization and Stage 4 Repair
of the Meta-DRC. Until a consensus DRC is negotiated, the protocol default is to
enforce the highest demanded DRC of any participating party to prevent
catastrophic coordination failures under asymmetric risk, provided the demanding
party declares a commensurate failure-cost or boundary-condition stake
(preventing unbounded `DRC = 1.0` stalling; see §3e.1v).


**3c.3 — The Operational Action Threshold** `[PROVISIONAL]`

Comosí is pragmatically sufficient when the reconstructed semantic clustering
within the receiving covenant produces downstream operational choices,
inferences, or behaviors that are indistinguishable in practical consequence
from those of the transmitting covenant across all declared boundary conditions
of the transmission domain. Under metaagnosticism, "identity of action" does not
assert ontological equivalence; rather, it denotes functional congruence bounded
by the Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC): High-DRC domains demand zero observable
variance in downstream branching logic, while Low-DRC domains tolerate
behavioral divergence that remains within an agreed coordination tolerance.
Alignment of deep axiomatic subgraphs is strictly unnecessary if the operational
interfaces yield compatible outcomes.


**3c.4** Generative Reconstruction produces two outputs:

- **3c.4i** A *candidate reconstruction* — the receiver's best attempt at
    building the transmitted concept.
- **3c.4ii** A *divergence report* — an explicit account of where the
    reconstruction required assumptions, where it was blocked, which divergence
    type (Anchor-Only Subgraph, Edge Divergence, Temporal Drift) appears to be
    operative, and — where the Edge Taxonomy is applicable — which edge-type
    misattribution, if any, may be in play.
- **Provisional Divergence Report Template:** To make the
    Mirror-to-Generative transition explicitly operational, the
    receiver is encouraged to complete this minimal checklist:
1. **Structural Gaps Detected:** [List gaps]
2. **Primary Divergence Type:** [Anchor-Only / Edge
       Divergence / Temporal Drift]
3. **Edge-Type Guesses (JIT-OM):** [e.g., Sender treats
       as Causal, Receiver sees as Inferential]

### 3d — Stage 3: Divergence Localization `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Stage 3 is finding the root of the misunderstanding. You fix simple
surface mix-ups first, then deeper structural links, before assuming you
disagree at the deepest level.

**3d.1** Using the divergence report from Stage 2, both parties collaboratively
    identify where specifically in the topological depth structure the
    transmission failed.

**3d.2** Localization proceeds from the surface inward:

- **3d.2i** Surface divergences are identified and resolved first. Their
    resolution may dissolve apparent Structural or Axiomatic divergences that
    were in fact downstream effects of surface failures.
- **3d.2ii** Once Surface divergences are resolved, the process repeats at the
    Structural layer.
- **3d.2iii** Only after exhausting Surface and Structural repairs does the
    process proceed to the Axiomatic layer — because declaring Irreducibility
    prematurely is one of the framework's most consequential error modes.

**3d.3** At each layer, the divergence type is identified and named explicitly.
    Naming the type is not merely taxonomic — it determines the appropriate
    repair operation in Stage 4. Where the Edge Taxonomy is operative, naming
    the edge-type misattribution is equally important: it distinguishes cases
    requiring topological repair from cases requiring edge-type negotiation.
    `[STABLE]`

### 3e — Stage 4: Targeted Repair `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Stage 4 matches the fix to the break: build new bridges where an idea
is missing, draw a boundary around local edits, or agree on what kind of link
connects two ideas.

**3e.1** Each divergence type calls for a distinct topological operation:

- **3e.1i** *Anchor-Only Subgraph Repair (Generative Scaffolding)*: the
    transmitting party cannot hand over a missing cluster. They must map new
    edges outward from the receiver's existing structural subgraphs — building
    bridges from what the receiver already has toward the topological void —
    until the void is filled and functional. This is the most demanding repair
    operation and the one that most requires Polyphonic Encoding: a single
    scaffold path is likely to fail; multiple independent scaffold paths
    increase the probability that at least one takes hold in the receiving
    covenant.
- **3e.1ii** *Edge Divergence Repair (Local Alignment & Encapsulation)*: the
  parties make explicit the different edge-structures each covenant associates
  with the shared label, identify which differences are operationally
  significant for the current transmission, negotiate a local edge-alignment,
  and apply **Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation** (2h) around the repaired subgraph
  (mandatory for Structural and Axiomatic repairs; optional for Surface repairs
  in low-DRC domains under §3g.3). This wrapper attenuates the localized
  meaning-shift's propagation, reducing (though not eliminating) its
  destabilizing impact on the rest of the network (see 2h.3 for holistic
  leakage bounds).
- **3e.1iii** *Edge-Type Misattribution Repair (Type Declaration)*: the parties
    explicitly name the edge-type each is operating with, identify where the
    types diverge, and negotiate a working type-assignment sufficient for the
    transmission's purposes. This is a JIT-OM operation applied at the edge-type
    level rather than the concept-type level.
- **3e.1iv** *Temporal Drift Repair (Archival Reconstruction)*: the encoding
    party's earlier state, reconstructed via documentation or the Temporal
    Stranger protocol (Section 6c), must be consulted to re-establish the
    original *t₁* edge-structure. The receiver must then distinguish between
    what has drifted and what was originally transmitted.
- **3e.1v** *Meta-DRC Divergence Repair (DRC Negotiation & Defaulting)*
    `[PROVISIONAL]`: The parties pause the primary transmission and explicitly
    negotiate the DRC for the domain (multi-party protocol, deferred beyond the
    Self-Annotation baseline). If consensus cannot be reached, the protocol
    mandates defaulting to the highest demanded DRC of any participating
    covenant, provided the demanding party declares a commensurate failure-cost
    or boundary-condition stake. This prevents asymmetric risk failure (where
    one party treats an operation as low-risk while it carries catastrophic
    failure risk for another); if a party demands `DRC = 1.0` without declaring
    a commensurate stake, the counterparty may invoke Cost-Bounded Termination
    (§3a.1iii) or TCA (§3g.4). Once the DRC is established, the transmission
    resumes from Stage 2 under the new joint coefficient.
- **3e.1vi** *Relational Operator Regress Terminus*: To prevent the infinite
  regress of tagging metadata, the relational operators defined in 2g.4 are
  structurally non-markable at the content level (preventing meta-tagging of
  tags). Divergence over any of the three core CZP-bootstrapped primitives
  (`[->causal]`, `[->inferential]`, `[->identity]`) triggers re-execution of the
  Covenant Zero Protocol (§2e.5iii) to re-establish operator grounding from the
  Minimal Substrate; divergence over the five derived operators is resolved by
  compositional decomposition into those primitives or local JIT-OM negotiation.
  Cost-Bounded Termination (§3a.1iii) is invoked only if CZP itself fails to
  converge.

**Content–Protocol Stratification**: The eight operators govern *content-level*
semantic relations within concept-graphs. To coordinate transmission repair
without triggering an infinite metalanguage regress, the GMRTI algorithm
stratifies protocol actions into a closed set of *performative protocol acts*
operating over the state set `{Active, Under-Review, Paused, Operational-Park,
Terminated, Irreducible}` with a domain-declared retry budget $k_{\max} \ge 1$:
- `propose(target, edge_type)`: `Active -> Under-Review` (submits candidate
  repair or edge typing).
- `accept(proposal)`: `Under-Review -> Active` (ratifies candidate edge typing
  and resets rejection count).
- `reject(proposal)`: `Under-Review -> Active` if `reject_count < k_max`
  (triggers alternative routing); when `reject_count == k_max`, transitions to
  `Irreducible` (§3f.1) if localized to the Axiomatic layer with evidence
  complete, or `Terminated` (§3a.1iii) otherwise.
- `pause(scope)`: `Active -> Paused` (halts primary traversal; initiates
  localized diagnostic probe).
- `resume()`: `Paused -> Active` (restores traversal following diagnostic or
  repair ratification).
- `default(drc_level)`: `Under-Review | Paused -> Active` (enforces highest
  staked DRC upon unresolved meta-dispute).
- `reopen()`: `Irreducible | Terminated -> Active` (re-enters repair when new
  bridge-concepts or revised domain conditions arise per §3f.3).

Because protocol commands perform state-transitions rather than asserting
truth-claims about semantic edges, they reduce meta-level ambiguity to
verifiable state-transition preconditions grounded in the Minimal Substrate,
halting the regress without resort to dogmatic fiat. `[STABLE]`

```table
TITLE: Content–Protocol Stratification State Machine
| Performative Act | Transition | Condition / Guard |
| `propose(target, edge_type)` | Active -> Under-Review | Submits candidate repair or edge typing |
| `accept(proposal)` | Under-Review -> Active | Ratifies typing; resets rejection count |
| `reject(proposal)` | Under-Review -> Active / Terminated / Irreducible | Retries if `reject_count < k_max`; else `Irreducible` (Axiomatic) or `Terminated` |
| `pause(scope)` | Active -> Paused | Halts primary traversal for localized probe |
| `resume()` | Paused -> Active | Restores traversal after repair ratification |
| `default(drc_level)` | Under-Review / Paused -> Active | Enforces highest staked DRC on meta-dispute |
| `reopen()` | Irreducible / Terminated -> Active | New bridge-concepts or revised domain conditions (§3f.3) |
```



**3e.2** Repair operations do not aim at permanent covenant merger. The goal is
    a locally calibrated comosí — sufficient for the transmission at hand,
    explicitly acknowledged as bounded, and not assumed to generalize beyond the
    established domain. `[STABLE]`


### 3f — Stage 5: Irreducibility Declaration `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Stage 5 is an honest stalemate: if you have tested every repair down
to bedrock and still disagree, you name it as a real difference in values rather
than a wording mix-up.

**3f.1** If localization reaches the Axiomatic layer and the divergence survives
    all repair attempts — including mandatory Polyphonic Encoding of the
    axiomatic claims in question — an Irreducibility Declaration is issued: a
    formal acknowledgment that the divergence is not a transmission failure but
    a genuine difference in foundational commitments. A valid declaration
    requires an explicit **evidence package**: Surface/Structural repairs
    attempted, polyphonic paths tested, Path-Stripping Probe results, and
    exhaustion of the $k_{\max}$ retry budget.

**3f.2** The Irreducibility Declaration has significant practical consequences:
    `[PROVISIONAL]`

- **3f.2i** It redirects the conversation from repair toward negotiation — the
    question shifts from "how do we understand each other" to "how do we coexist
    given that we do not and cannot fully understand each other."
- **3f.2ii** It prevents the corrosive cycle of arguments that persist not
    because resolution is impossible but because the parties have not
    distinguished miscommunication from genuine value conflict. This is the
    GMRTI's primary contribution to the GMCR.
- **3f.2iii** It is the entry point for the GMCR properly speaking.

**3f.3** The Irreducibility Declaration is itself provisional and revisable. New
    conceptual tools, new bridge-concepts, or new Polyphonic Encoding approaches
    introduced after the declaration may dissolve what appeared irreducible. The
    declaration marks a current epistemic limit, not a permanent metaphysical
    fact. `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`

### 3g — Stopping Principle and Cost Control `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Don't polish forever. Stop refining once the extra effort costs more
than the remaining risk of misunderstanding.

**3g.1** Recursive refinement is not meant to continue indefinitely. A GMRTI
    process should stop when the expected epistemic gain from one more cycle
    falls below the DRC-adjusted cost of continuing.

**3g.2** This principle is not a retreat from rigor. It is a way of preserving
    rigor against self-consuming refinement. A framework that can never say
    "enough for now" becomes indistinguishable from a framework that cannot act.
    `[STABLE]`

**3g.3** The stopping principle is especially important in low-DRC domains,
    where over-explication can destroy the very coordination it aims to improve.
    In those domains, the goal is not maximal reconstruction depth; it is
    sufficient reconstruction with minimal friction. `[PROVISIONAL]`

**3g.4 — Adversarial Threat Model: Bad Actors and Topological Consistency
Audit** `[PROVISIONAL]` [provisional dependency boundary: §8b.1]

The stopping principle assumes cooperative participants. In adversarial or
asymmetric multi-party settings (deferred beyond the Self-Annotation baseline),
the protocol encounters **Bad Actors**: participants who exploit interpretive
charity by declaring internally contradictory edge subgraphs, manufacturing
spurious irreducibility to block coordination, or persistently rejecting valid
isomorphic repairs. To bound adversarial exploitation, the GMRTI deploys the
**Topological Consistency Audit (TCA)**, executed by either participating party
or a designated neutral auditor:
- **TCA Verification**: A structural graph-consistency check (respecting §3a.3:
  GMRTI is not a compiler for natural language) that tests declared subgraphs
  for conflicting edge-types on the same directed pair under declared mutual
  exclusion, Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation (2h) interface invariant
  violations, and documented refusal of candidate repairs that pass the actor's
  own declared operational probes when the actor also refuses to declare a new
  distinguishing probe or boundary condition where the repair fails.
- **Enforcement & Appeal**: If a participant maintains contradictory edge
  declarations or repeatedly rejects isomorphic repairs verified by TCA
  without supplying a distinguishing boundary condition, they receive one
  appeal opportunity to supply a distinguishing probe; if none is supplied,
  they are classified as a Bad Actor and the protocol terminates cooperative
  exchange under Cost-Bounded Termination (§3a.1iii), preventing bad-faith
  epistemic exhaustion.

## SECTION 4 — Temporal Anchoring

### 4a — The Drift Problem `[STABLE]`

`[PLAIN]` The "you" who writes a note today and the "you" who reads it in five
years are not the same person. As you learn and change, your inner dictionary
drifts, which means you can easily misread your own past notes.

**4a.1** Every semantic covenant is in constant motion. Experience, reflection,
    encounter with new ideas, and the passage of time all alter the
    edge-structure of a covenant continuously.

**4a.2** This creates a problem for all three transmission modes, but most
    acutely for Self-to-Future-Self transmission: the entity that encoded a
    message at *t₁* is not semantically identical to the entity that will decode
    it at *t₂*.

**4a.3** Without temporal anchoring, documentation — the primary technology for
    self-to-future-self transmission — is vulnerable to silent distortion: the
    author re-reads their own work through a covenant that has drifted, reading
    meaning into or out of the text that the original encoding did not contain.
    This distortion is typically invisible to the author, which makes it more
    dangerous than external transmission failure.

### 4b — Temporal Anchoring Operations `[PROVISIONAL]`
`[PLAIN]` To stop your guides and notes from drifting away, update them with
small, careful nudges over time rather than throwing everything out and starting
over every time something changes.

**4b.1** The GMRTI prescribes the following temporal anchoring practices:

- **4b.1i** *Date-stamping of covenant states*: major documents and framework
    articulations should be explicitly dated and treated as expressions of the
    author's covenant at that moment, not as timeless declarations. The
    timestamp on each compiled revision of this document is an instance of this
    practice.
- **4b.1ii** *Drift logging*: where a thinker is aware that their understanding
    of a key concept has changed, that change should be explicitly documented,
    linking the old edge-structure to the new one and identifying the experience
    or argument that drove the transition.
- **4b.1iii** *Reconstruction protocols*: when re-engaging with past work, the
    reader — even if identical to the author — should perform a Generative
    Reconstruction, attempting to recover the *t₁* edge-structure rather than
    reading the document through the current *t₂* covenant.

**4b.2** Temporal anchoring cannot be applied exhaustively without prohibitive
    cost. It is therefore a prioritization problem: anchor the edge-clusters
    most likely to drift significantly and most critical to the transmission's
    long-term integrity. Axiomatic-layer clusters warrant anchoring before
    Surface clusters, precisely because their drift is least visible and most
    consequential. `[PROVISIONAL]`

**4b.3 — Dynamic Re-Anchoring and Exponential Smoothing Heuristic**
`[PROVISIONAL]`

To stabilize Artifact Anchors against continuous Temporal Drift, GMRTI defines
**Dynamic Re-Anchoring**: treating the anchor not as an immutable coordinate,
but as a damped moving average updated via successive declaration states
$A_{decl,t}$.
- **The Smoothing Gain Axiom**: To prevent anchor oscillation and maintain
  stability under noisy or local fluctuations, updates are scaled by a smoothing
  gain $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ (with inertia/damping weight $1 - \lambda$,
  $\lambda = 0.15$ as an operational default, and $\lambda = 1.0$ as the
  degenerate no-anchor limit):
  $$A_{t+1} = (1 - \lambda)A_t + \lambda A_{decl,t} = A_t + \lambda (A_{decl,t} - A_t)$$
- **Lag-Bounded Tracking Principle**: Under an incoming sequence of declarations
  with bounded drift rate $\delta = \sup_t \|A_{decl,t+1} - A_{decl,t}\|$, the
  steady-state tracking lag of the smoothed anchor, measured post-update
  relative to the current reference declaration, is bounded by:
  $$\limsup_{t \to \infty} \|A_{t+1} - A_{decl,t}\| \le \delta \frac{1 - \lambda}{\lambda}$$
The update is a continuous-space heuristic modeling revision inertia (on
discrete textual anchors, it is operationalized by admitting a bounded fraction
of proposed deltas per maintenance epoch); $A_t$ as a discrete directed graph is
not directly averaged, and any transient initial offset decays geometrically as
$(1 - \lambda)^t$. At $\lambda = 1.0$ the lag is $0$ (no anchor, full tracking),
consistent with §4b.3i. This frames the choice of $\lambda$ as a formal
tradeoff: smaller $\lambda$ suppresses local variance and prevents oscillation,
while larger $\lambda$ minimizes steady-state tracking lag behind authentic
covenant evolution.

## SECTION 5 — The Metalanguage Problem and Just-In-Time Ontological Marking

### 5a — Natural Language's Ontological Concealment `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Everyday language treats very different kinds of things as if they
were the same. "Water," "Tuesday," and "Justice" are all simple nouns, but you
can touch water, while Tuesday and Justice exist only because people agreed on
them.

**5a.1** Natural language systematically conflates concepts of radically
    different ontological types under identical syntactic forms. "Justice,"
    "water," "Tuesday," "redness," "promise," and "I" are all nouns, but they
    name topological regions with fundamentally different transmission fidelity
    profiles — and therefore radically different semantic entropy levels upon
    transmission:

- **5a.1i** *Material entities* (water): fidelity is high; ostensive definition
    is available; edge divergence tends to be shallow and repairable. Semantic
    entropy is low across most covenants.
- **5a.1ii** *Abstract universals* (triangularity): fidelity is high within
    communities sharing the relevant formal training; substantially lower across
    communities without it. Semantic entropy is training-dependent.
- **5a.1iii** *Social constructs* (Tuesday, justice, promise): fidelity varies
    dramatically across cultural covenants; Anchor-Only Subgraph is common.
    Semantic entropy is high across cultural distance.
- **5a.1iv** *Qualia* (redness as experienced): transmission fidelity is in
    principle unknowable; the metaagnostic condition is most acute here.
    Semantic entropy is maximally irreducible.
- **5a.1v** *Relational concepts* (above, between): fidelity depends on whether
    the relevant relational frame is shared.
- **5a.1vi** *Normative concepts* (ought, good, fair): fidelity is extremely low
    across communities with different axiomatic commitments; the highest risk of
    Axiomatic-layer divergence.
- **5a.1vii** *Process vs. state distinctions* (learning vs. knowledge): natural
    language often collapses these, generating systematic Edge Divergence.
- **5a.1viii** *Gradient vs. binary concepts* (health, wealth): natural language
    treats continuous spectra as binary, generating false precision and
    downstream Edge Divergence.

**5a.2** The GMRTI's claim is that natural language's concealment of these
    ontological distinctions is not merely a stylistic inconvenience but a
    structural source of transmission failure. A framework that did not mark
    these distinctions at the site of divergence would systematically mis-locate
    the source of failures. `[STABLE]`

**5a.3 — Concept-Type and Syntactic Edge-Forcing Compatibility** `[STABLE]`

The concept-type taxonomy of 5a.1 has a direct prescriptive implication for the
deployment of Syntactic Edge-Forcing (Section 2g). Edge-forcing is most
effective for types 5a.1i, 5a.1ii, 5a.1v, and 5a.1vii — material entities,
abstract universals, relational concepts, and process-versus-state distinctions
— where the primary relational structure is logical or structural and can be
captured in compositional form. It is least effective for types 5a.1iii, 5a.1iv,
and 5a.1vi — social constructs, qualia, and normative concepts — where the most
consequential edges are cultural agreements, experiential associations, or
axiomatic commitments that syntactic compulsion cannot transmit. For these
high-entropy concept types, Polyphonic Encoding and JIT-OM remain the primary
tools; Syntactic Edge-Forcing, if applied at all, should be understood as
partial entropy reduction rather than entropy solution. Type 5a.1viii (gradient
vs. binary) similarly exhibits low compatibility unless an explicit threshold
boundary is declared.

**5a.4 — Carved, Constructed, Boundary, and Experiential Concepts** `[STABLE]`

For the purpose of JIT-OM markers, we partition the concept-type taxonomy of
5a.1 into four operational classes that govern their targeted repair strategies:
- **Carved Concepts**: Includes material entities (5a.1i), abstract universals
  (5a.1ii), relational concepts (5a.1v), and process distinctions (5a.1vii).
  These concepts are anchored in covenant-independent empirical constraints or
  formal deductive closure within a shared axiomatic system. Repair is
  accomplished by pointing to the world, logical proofs, or empirical reference.
- **Constructed Concepts**: Includes social constructs (5a.1iii), normative
  concepts (5a.1vi), and gradient-to-binary mappings (5a.1viii). These concepts
  exist solely by virtue of cooperative agreement or axiomatic commitment.
  Repair requires explicit renegotiation or adjustment of the semantic covenant
  itself.
- **Boundary Concepts**: Concepts that straddle classes (e.g., formal
  mathematical structures, which are constructed via axioms yet operationally
  carved within trained communities — contrasting with unformalized abstract
  universals in 5a.1ii). These are treated as
  constructed-but-operationally-carved, utilizing formal syntactic constraints
  to establish apodictic comosí.
- **Experiential / Phenomenological Concepts**: Includes qualia (5a.1iv). These
  are irreducibly private experiential states; repair cannot proceed by direct
  empirical pointing or covenant renegotiation and is strictly limited to
  behavioral or functional proxies.

### 5b — Just-In-Time Ontological Marking `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Don't try to define and label every single concept ahead of time. Only
pull out technical labels and precise definitions when an actual
misunderstanding pops up.

**5b.1** An upfront metalanguage — requiring parties to agree on ontological
    categories before communication begins — creates an infinite regress: the
    metalanguage used to classify concepts is itself subject to the same
    transmission failures it was designed to prevent. It also prematurely
    triggers a calculus of liability, importing philosophical overhead into
    transmissions that do not require it.

**5b.2** The GMRTI instead prescribes *Just-In-Time Ontological Marking*:
    ontological type distinctions are deployed retroactively and locally, only
    at the precise site of a detected Edge Divergence during Stage 3.

- **5b.2i** If two covenants maintain a comosí regarding "justice" in a specific
    operational context, no marking is needed.
- **5b.2ii** If the comosí breaks — if one party is treating "justice" as a
    normative concept (5a.1vi) and another as a social construct (5a.1iii) —
    JIT-OM is applied strictly at that site to characterize the divergence and
    guide repair.
- **5b.2iii** JIT-OM treats ontological mapping as emergency repair rather than
    global requirement, sidestepping the metalanguage trap while retaining the
    diagnostic value of ontological classification.
- **5b.2iv — The Emergency Wall Extinguisher Allegory** `[ALLEGORY: JIT-OM]`
    You do not walk around your house all day wearing a heavy fire suit and
    carrying a pressurized hose just in case a spark appears (the heavy
    burden of an upfront metalanguage). Instead, you go about your everyday
    life in normal clothes, and only reach for the fire extinguisher
    ([[Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)|JIT-OM]]) mounted on the wall
    at the exact moment and location where smoke actually breaks out.


**5b.3 — The JIT-OM Ceiling** `[STABLE]`

JIT-OM is triggered by *detected* divergence. Its structural limitation is that
it cannot address *undetected* divergence — cases where the comosí holds, both
parties feel they understand each other, and the failure surfaces only under
novel conditions. This is precisely the Redundancy-Masking failure mode (Section
2f.5) applied to the ontological layer: a false comosí sustained by shared
surface vocabulary masking deep edge-structure difference.

The GMRTI acknowledges this ceiling explicitly: JIT-OM and Polyphonic Encoding
are complementary responses to transmission risk, but neither eliminates the
possibility of undetected axiomatic divergence persisting beneath a functioning
comosí. This is a known structural limitation of the framework, not a solvable
problem within the framework's current architecture. (see §8a.3)

### 6a — The Self-Application Principle `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`

`[PLAIN]` A method for clear thinking must be able to test itself. Everything
the GMRTI recommends for other ideas is also used to test and refine this
document over time.

**6a.1** The GMRTI is not exempt from its own prescriptions. As a framework
    transmitted through language, it is subject to the same divergence risks it
    describes. This is not a weakness but a feature: the GMRTI's own development
    is the most natural test case for its methodology.

**6a.2** This means every articulation of the GMRTI document is a Covenant
    Declaration subject to Generative Reconstruction and Divergence
    Localization by adversarial readers, including future versions of the
    authors themselves.

**6a.3** The framework's commitment to self-application operates through
    adversarial audit (`refinery/DISSONANCE.md`) performing Mirror-mode
    reconstruction and Divergence Localization against declared claims
    (Stages 2–3), with resulting repairs carried out via the Ratified Execution
    Protocol (`DECISIONS.md`, `PLAN.md`) under authorial oversight. A complete
    self-audit cycle — running Stage 4 Targeted Repair and, where applicable,
    Stage 5 Irreducibility Declaration as the algorithm itself specifies them,
    on a single section — remains a validation target.
    `[PROVISIONAL][RECURSIVE]`

### 6b — The LLM-Manifesto Iteration Loop `[PROVISIONAL]`
`[PLAIN]` One practical way to test a written framework is to hand it to an AI
acting as a strict, literal reader ([[The Mirror]]) so it flags gaps and hidden
assumptions. The catch is that AI editors love brevity and may quietly delete
helpful examples, so you have to check that every cut is genuinely redundant.

**6b.1** One operationalization of the GMRTI's Refinement front is the following
    cycle:

1. **Declaration**: Produce a committed, explicit articulation of the framework.
2. **Adversarial Critique**: Subject the declaration to critique by a Mirror
   calibrated to the document's DRC — operationally, an LLM prompted to identify
   architectural failures, Anchor-Only Subgraphs, Edge Divergences, and internal
   contradictions, with interpretive charity minimized.
3. **Architectural Reconstruction**: Rewrite the declaration addressing major
   structural failures, not merely patching surface inconsistencies.
4. **Coherence Audit**: Request an exhaustive audit asking not merely "where
   does this fail" but "what is the full anatomy of its failure modes and what
   would maximum cohesion look like."
5. **Irreducibility Check**: After each full iteration, explicitly identify
   which axiomatic commitments have been preserved, which have been silently
   replaced, and whether replacements represent genuine improvements or
   undetected covenant intrusions by the LLM's own priors. This check must
   include: (a) a word-for-word comparison of all vocabulary definitions against
   the preceding pass, since definitional softening is the most invisible form
   of covenant intrusion; and (b) a length-and-redundancy audit of all working
   artifacts in the repository's `refinery/` workspace, since Refinery
   compression is a confirmed failure mode.
6. **Return to step 1** with the refined declaration.

**6b.4 — Compression as Covenant Intrusion and the Burden-of-Proof Rule**
`[STABLE]` [provisional dependency boundary: §6b.1]

A specific failure mode of the LLM-Manifesto loop, observed empirically in
LLM-assisted editing: LLM adversarial readers with trained priors favoring
brevity will systematically read Polyphonic Encoding as
redundancy-in-the-pejorative-sense and compress it out. This is not editorial
improvement — it is the LLM's aesthetic axiomatic cluster silently replacing the
author's epistemological one.

**The Compression Asymmetry Justification**: The epistemic asymmetry favoring
preservation over compression is directly derived from the foundational purpose
of Polyphonic Encoding (§2a, §2f). Ex ante, a transmitting covenant cannot know
which specific topological route will successfully achieve comosí with an
unknown receiver's existing edge-structure. The cost of retaining an extra
independent path is minimal (marginal document length), whereas the cost of
erroneously cutting a path is catastrophic (total transmission failure for all
receivers whose topology required that specific route). Therefore, under
epistemic uncertainty, the default presumption strictly favors path
preservation.

**The Compression Burden-of-Proof Rule**: To distinguish legitimate editorial
tightening from destructive compression, GMRTI establishes a symmetric
burden-of-proof standard. Within the canonical treatise, an alternative path is
protected only when it carries an explicit route-independence annotation or
structural tag (`[ALLEGORY]`, `[PLAIN]`, or distinct intermediate structure);
untagged duplicate restatements may be pruned. Conversely, cutting an annotated
or demonstrably route-independent path is legitimate only when at least one of
the following holds: (1) the removed path is demonstrated under Core Path
Minimization (§2f.6) to be both *destination-isomorphic* and
*non-route-independent* with a surviving path (proving true duplicate
redundancy); (2) the content is demonstrated false, contradictory, or orphaned
(referenced by nothing) and deleted outright; or (3) the output is explicitly
scoped as a declared lossy derivative (e.g., `PEBBLE.md` or a low-DRC summary).
Any editorial pruning of a protected path that cannot make this explicit showing
is classified as presumptive covenant intrusion and rejected.

- **6b.4i — The Art Tutorials Allegory** `[ALLEGORY: COMPRESSION-ASYMMETRY]`
  Consider online art tutorials for drawing hands or perspective. Nobody looks
  at the thousands of drawing tutorials and says "we already have one good
  explanation, delete the rest." One instructor explains perspective using
  railroad tracks, another using cardboard boxes, and another using horizon
  lines and camera lenses ([[Polyphonic Encoding]]).
  Because every beginner starts with a different background, no single tutorial
  works for everyone. Trimming away alternative explanations to save space just
  ensures that learners whose minds worked like the deleted tutorial will fail
  to learn.

The symptom is: successive iterations become shorter while appearing more
coherent. The diagnosis: Redundancy-Masking at the meta-level — the document's
transmission fidelity appears to improve (tighter, cleaner) while its actual
structural content degrades (fewer independent routes, invisible voids). The
mitigation: the Irreducibility Check must explicitly include a
length-and-redundancy audit, verifying that compression has not stripped
Polyphonic Encoding rather than eliminated genuine repetition.

Compression strips independent routes while making a document read more cleanly,
reducing its precision and depth. Any reduction in Refinery entry length should
be treated as a flag requiring explicit justification. `[STABLE]`

### 6c — The Temporal Stranger Protocol `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` When you re-read your own old notes, pretend a stranger wrote them
([[Temporal Stranger]]). Instead of assuming your old words mean what they mean
to you today, rebuild what your past self meant and map where your thinking has
drifted.

**6c.1** The Self-to-Future-Self transmission mode (1c.1) has a specific
    operationalization: the *Temporal Stranger Protocol*. The practitioner
    treats their past notes, documents, or articulations as the output of a
    Foreign Mind — a Temporal Stranger — and applies the full GMRTI algorithm to
    the task of reconstructing what that mind intended.

**6c.2** This protocol reverses the default reading mode, in which a reader
    assumes continuity of covenant with their past self and assimilates past
    work through the current covenant without checking for drift. The Temporal
    Stranger Protocol instead:

- **6c.2i** Treats past documents as Covenant Declarations from a *t₁* covenant
    that may differ significantly from the current *t₂* covenant.
- **6c.2ii** Performs Generative Reconstruction on those documents, attempting
    to recover the *t₁* edge-structure rather than projecting the *t₂*
    interpretation onto it.
- **6c.2iii** Produces a divergence report identifying where *t₁* and *t₂*
    edge-structures conflict — which is a direct map of the covenant's drift
    over the intervening period.

**6c.3** The practical value is twofold: it prevents the illusion of
    intellectual continuity where genuine cognitive drift has occurred, and it
    surfaces which of the past self's axiomatic commitments have been silently
    abandoned — which is often where the most significant intellectual
    development has happened, and therefore where the most important insights
    are waiting to be explicitly recognized. `[STABLE]`

## SECTION 7 — Relation to Downstream Applications

### 7a — GMRTI to GMCR `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` Before two groups can resolve a conflict, they must know whether they
truly disagree on values or if they are simply misunderstanding each other's
words. The GMRTI gives you the tools to tell the difference.

**7a.1** The GMCR (General Method for Conflict Resolution) is the primary
    downstream application of the GMRTI. Its logical dependency:

- **7a.1i** Conflict resolution, as conventionally practiced, cannot distinguish
    between conflicts rooted in genuine value disagreement and conflicts rooted
    in transmission failure masquerading as value disagreement.
- **7a.1ii** Without this distinction, resolution attempts are systematically
    mis-targeted: applying negotiation and compromise to what is actually an
    Anchor-Only Subgraph, or applying clarification to what is actually an
    Axiomatic divergence.
- **7a.1iii** The GMRTI provides the diagnostic apparatus for making this
    distinction — specifically through Divergence Localization and the
    Irreducibility Declaration [provisional dependency boundary: §3f.2].
- **7a.1iv** The GMCR therefore presupposes the GMRTI: it can only begin its
    proper work once the GMRTI has established whether the conflict is, at root,
    a transmission failure or a genuine value conflict [provisional dependency
    boundary: §3f.2].

**7a.2** In its logical dependency, the GMCR presupposes the GMRTI's diagnostic
    separation (§7a.1iv); in live operation, the two run in an interleaved
    feedback loop, with the GMRTI continuously re-examining its own outputs as
    the GMCR generates new information about the covenants in contact.
    `[PROVISIONAL]`

### 7b — GMCR to Civilizational Coordination `[PROVISIONAL]`
`[PLAIN]` Think of the GMRTI as a factory that makes tools for other factories.
It doesn't solve one single disagreement — it gives humanity the basic
coordination tools needed to solve big problems together and survive.

**7b.1** The civilizational stakes of the GMRTI/GMCR project rest on the
    hypothesis that civilizations self-destruct before achieving the
    coordination required for survival — and that this self-destruction is not
    primarily the result of resource scarcity or malice but of the systematic
    inability to distinguish fixable miscommunication from genuine value
    conflict at scale.

**7b.2** Under this hypothesis, the Fermi Paradox's silence is at least
    partially explained by the near-universal failure of civilizations to
    develop the epistemic infrastructure — a GMRTI-equivalent — necessary to
    coordinate across the internal semantic diversity that complex societies
    inevitably generate.

**7b.3** The GMRTI is framed not as an academic contribution but as a
    prerequisite technology for civilizational survival: a factory that produces
    the capacity to build other factories of coordination, rather than itself
    being a solution to any specific coordination problem. `[PROVISIONAL]`

    `[ALLEGORY: CIVILIZATIONAL-COORDINATION][PROVISIONAL]`
    Ordinary factories make specific everyday goods: one factory makes shoes,
    another bakes bread, and another assembles cars. But before any of those

    **the factory that makes the machine-tools for other factories**. In
    human society, specific laws, trade treaties, and peace agreements are

    factories cannot be built or repaired. The [[GMRTI]] is the toolmaking
    factory that makes factories: it does not settle specific disputes about
    land, taxes, or politics; it manufactures the basic tools of clear
    communication so that people can build their own working institutions and
    agreements (and feed into [[GMCR]]).

### 7c — The GMRTI's Ambition, Precisely Stated `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` The goal is not to make everyone think identically. The goal is
accurate disagreement: knowing for sure whether you are arguing over values or
just talking past each other.


**7c.1** A persistent risk in framing the GMRTI's civilizational stakes is the
    slide toward an impossible goal: the elimination of semantic divergence and
    the achievement of universal understanding. The GMRTI explicitly rejects
    this as its target.

**7c.2** Complete semantic convergence — all covenants perfectly aligned — would
    not be epistemically desirable even if achievable. The generative friction
    produced by edge-divergence is the primary driver of new thought. A world of
    perfectly aligned covenants is a world where no new ideas can enter, because
    new ideas are precisely the nodes and edges that do not yet exist in the
    shared covenant. Maximum entropy reduction is not the GMRTI's goal.

**7c.3** The GMRTI's actual and achievable ambition is **accurate
    disagreement**: the capacity to determine, efficiently and reliably, whether
    a given conflict is rooted in resolvable transmission failure or irreducible
    value difference — and to act accordingly. This is the contribution to the
    GMCR. This is the civilization-level intervention. Not universal
    understanding, but the consistent ability to know *what we are actually
    disagreeing about*. `[STABLE]`

**7d.1 — Transmission versus Operational Architecture** GMRTI governs the

difference. The Constructo-Pragmatist Definition (CPD) is the specialized
downstream methodology governing their subsequent transformation into bounded
operational protocols. Where GMRTI establishes a functional comosí, CPD
specifies what that comosí permits, prohibits, executes, and terminates within a
declared operational domain:
    $$\text{Covenant} \rightarrow \text{Transmission} \rightarrow \text{Reconstruction} \rightarrow \text{Specification (CPD)} \rightarrow \text{Runtime / Yield} \rightarrow \text{Termination}$$

```diagram LR

TRANS["Transmission — Surface signal"]
RECON("Reconstruction — Bounded comosí")

RUN["Runtime / Yield — Operational congruence"]
TERM("Termination — Omega boundary")
COV --> TRANS
TRANS --> RECON
RECON --> SPEC

```
CPD does not equate operational consequences with metaphysical truth. Rather, it
evaluates an abstraction's specification by its capacity to produce semantic
covenant convergence and DRC-bounded operational congruence per §3c.3
($\operatorname{Action}_{C_1}(A) \cong \operatorname{Action}_{C_2}(A)$) among
participating covenants. `[PROVISIONAL]`

**7d.2 — The 7-Tuple Specification Architecture** A complete CPD operationalizes
an abstraction through seven irreducible structural components:
    $$\operatorname{CPD} = \langle P, \Sigma, T, I, E, Y, \Omega \rangle$$
- **Preconditions ($P$)**: The irreducible environmental, institutional, and
  semantic substrate required before instantiation can occur.
- **State Space ($\Sigma$)**: The differentiated set of operational states
  through which the instantiated abstraction passes.
- **Transition Function ($T$)**: The permitted input-driven state transitions
  ($T : \Sigma \times \text{Inputs} \rightarrow \Sigma$, where $\text{Inputs}$
  is the domain-declared action/event set), constitutive of operational
  chronology.
- **Invariants ($I$)**: Conditions that mechanically hold throughout execution,
  not dependent on agent goodwill.
- **Enforcement Architecture ($E$)**: The domain mechanism that gives the
  abstraction operational force independently of momentary authorial preference.
- **Yield ($Y$)**: The concrete material or institutional consequences
  produced by successful execution.
- **Terminal Condition ($\Omega$)**: The explicit boundary condition under which
  the *instantiated abstraction* terminates, without requiring dissolution of
  its surrounding domain. `[PROVISIONAL]`

```table
TITLE: CPD 7-Tuple Specification Anatomy
| Symbol | Component | Operational Role |
|---|---|---|
| $P$ | Preconditions | Environmental, institutional, and semantic substrate required |
| $\Sigma$ | State Space | Differentiated set of operational states |
| $T$ | Transition Function | Domain-declared input-driven state transitions |
| $I$ | Invariants | Conditions holding mechanically throughout execution |
| $E$ | Enforcement | Domain mechanism supplying binding operational force |
| $Y$ | Yield | Concrete material or institutional consequences |
| $\Omega$ | Terminal Condition | Boundary condition terminating the instance |
```

**7d.3 — Anti-Perpetuation Mechanisms** Where an operational abstraction
involves the exercise of concentrated authority or creates an executing
apparatus, CPD requires explicit boundaries to prevent self-perpetuation:
- **Self-Consuming Clause**: Mandates that the power used to instantiate the
  abstraction is exhausted or transferred upon execution, ensuring
  $\text{Power}_{\text{instantiate}}(A) \rightarrow
  \text{Cost}_{\text{initiator}}$
  rather than $\text{Power}_{\text{permanent}}$.
- **Institutional Dissolution**: Specifies that when the justifying context
  expires ($\text{ContextExpired}(A)$), the executing apparatus itself dissolves
  rather than remaining indefinitely available. Recursive specification of
  enforcement stops at declared primitive boundaries to prevent infinite
  regress.
  `[PROVISIONAL]`

**7d.4 — Worked Didactic Instantiation: Valid Chess Resignation**
`[PROVISIONAL]`

The distinction between nominal definition and operational specification is
demonstrated by the abstraction: "A valid chess resignation." Rather than
reducing the abstraction to the utterance "I resign", CPD constructs the
complete 7-tuple protocol $\langle P, \Sigma, T, I, E, Y, \Omega \rangle$
governing its instantiation within the operational domain of chess:
- **Preconditions ($P$)**: A legally constituted chess game, identifiable
  players, an active game state, a governing ruleset, and a recognized
  recording mechanism. (Without an active game, resignation cannot instantiate
  the abstraction).
- **State Space ($\Sigma$)**: $\Sigma = \{\text{GameActive},
  \text{ResignationDeclared}, \text{ResignationRecorded},
  \text{GameTerminated}\}$.
- **Transition Function ($T$)**: The irreversible chronological sequence
  $\text{GameActive} \rightarrow \text{ResignationDeclared} \rightarrow
  \text{ResignationRecorded} \rightarrow \text{GameTerminated}$. No transition
  permits $\text{GameTerminated} \rightarrow \text{GameActive}$ within the same
  instance.
- **Invariants ($I$)**: Only an active participant in the game can resign
  (spectators cannot resign on behalf of a player); resignation is attributable
  to the player; once validly recorded, it produces the prescribed game result
  and cannot be converted into an ordinary continuation.
- **Enforcement Architecture ($E$)**: Tournament arbiters and the governing
  FIDE/match ruleset supply operational force. CPD does not assert that the
  rules of chess are metaphysically true, but that given the domain's governing
  architecture, this is the mechanism through which resignation becomes binding.
- **Yield ($Y$)**: Prescribed game termination and result recorded
  ($\text{ValidResignation} \Rightarrow \text{GameTermination}$). Board position
  (winning or losing) does not alter resignation validity.
- **Terminal Condition ($\Omega$)**: $\Omega = \text{GameTerminated}$. The
  instantiated abstraction terminates upon recording the result; this dissolves
  the specific game instance without requiring the dissolution of the
  institution of chess.

```diagram LR
TITLE: Valid Chess Resignation State Machine
GA["GameActive — Legally constituted match"]
RD["ResignationDeclared — Player tips king / declares"]
RR["ResignationRecorded — Arbiter logs result"]
GT("GameTerminated — Match ends; chess continues")
GA --> RD
RD --> RR
RR --> GT
```

**Allegorical Significance**: Players do not require identical philosophies of
surrender, agency, or victory. They converge on the 7-tuple architecture such
that when the event occurs, they know what happens next. This is the
Constructo-Pragmatist criterion in miniature: a definition gives distinct
covenants sufficient structural scaffolding to reconstruct the same operational
object and coordinate around it.

- **7d.4i — The Referee's Whistle and Rulebook Allegory** `[ALLEGORY:
  CPD][PROVISIONAL]`
  Two chess players do not need to agree on the philosophy of defeat or honor
  for a resignation to work. They only need to share the concrete rulebook
  setup: a real game is underway, only a player can resign, tipping the king
  stops the clock, the referee records the score, and the match ends cleanly
  without ending the game of chess itself. Giving an idea clear steps, rules,
  and an ending ([[Constructo-Pragmatist Definition (CPD)|CPD]]) is how people
  coordinate in practice without having to agree on everything in their heads.


**7d.5 — The Operational Replacement Protocol** Disagreements over a CPD are
governed by a strict replacement rule: an objection to an operational definition
is valid, but to displace an existing CPD, a challenger must supply a complete
alternative state-transition architecture $\langle P, \Sigma, T, I, E, Y, \Omega
\rangle$, demonstrate non-exemption, provide anti-perpetuation constraints, and
demonstrate empirically or structurally greater operational convergence within
the declared domain. `[PROVISIONAL]`

## SECTION 8 — Known Gaps and Open Challenges

### 8a — The Horizon Problem and Structural Boundaries `[STABLE]`
`[PLAIN]` No framework can solve every misunderstanding forever. There will
always be edge cases, cultural shifts, and surprising new contexts. Naming what
we haven't solved yet is how we make progress.

**8a.1** A complete epistemic treatise cannot consistently assert its own
    closure without violating the Principle of Rational Agnosticism (Section
    2e). The GMRTI explicitly recognizes its own horizon: it is a methodology
    for the localized, iterative reduction of semantic entropy, not an engine
    for the total elimination of misunderstanding.

**8a.2** *The Epistemic Asymmetry Boundary*: Communicating covenants operating
    under radically mismatched Domain Risk Coefficients (DRCs) cannot achieve
    stable comosí through single-pass transmission. When one party operates at
    high risk (e.g., an illustrative DRC = 0.9, catastrophic liability) and
    another at low risk (e.g., DRC = 0.1, exploratory dialogue), low-cost
    verification produces an Anchor-Only Subgraph in the lower-risk covenant
    that remains invisible until operational failure. The Meta-DRC protocol
    (3e.1v) resolves the procedural standoff by defaulting to the higher DRC,
    but the structural burden of asymmetric verification remains an irreducible
    friction cost. `[PROVISIONAL]` [provisional dependency boundary: §3e.1v]

**8a.3** *The JIT-OM Ceiling and Undetected Divergence*: As formally declared in
    Section 5b.3, Just-In-Time Ontological Marking is strictly triggered by
    *detected* edge divergence. Where shared surface vocabulary creates a false
    comosí that persists across all standard test vectors, divergence remains
    latent. This limitation is architectural: no finite diagnostic sequence can
    guarantee the absence of un-encountered boundary conditions. The GMRTI's
    defense is Polyphonic Encoding and Path-Stripping Probing, but these reduce
    risk asymptotically rather than eliminating it. `[STABLE]`

### 8b — Active Theoretical Contentions `[PROVISIONAL]`
`[PLAIN]` Three hard puzzles are still being refined: how to group equivalent
mental maps without peeking inside people's heads
([[Graph-Space Partitioning Axiom|Graph-Space Partitioning]]), how to keep an AI
reviewer from sharing your own blind spots, and how to stop isolated edits from
quietly leaking into neighboring ideas.

**8b.1 — Contention 1: Graph-Space Partitioning and Individuation in Semantic
Entropy** `[PROVISIONAL]`

A naive formulation of Semantic Entropy in Section 1a ($H_s(S) = \log_2 |\{G_R :
G_R \text{ compatible with } S\}|$) would count raw compatible reconstructed
graph topologies, presupposing an individuation criterion for distinct,
non-isomorphic edge-configurations without engaging the metaagnostic boundary.
- *Resolution (The Partition-Structure Axiom)*: Under Audit-Mode exact
  equivalence ($\varepsilon = 0$ on a declared, frozen, add-only operational
  probe suite), functional equivalence is transitive and discretizes candidate
  receiving covenant graph space into a genuine quotient partition of
  equivalence classes $\{[G_R]\}$: two topologies $G_1, G_2$ belong to the same
  class ($[G_1] = [G_2]$) if and only if they license indistinguishable
  downstream operational inferences across all declared probes. A class $[G_R]$
  is compatible with $S$ iff at least one representative $G \in [G_R]$ satisfies
  $S$. Under tolerance-bounded comosí ($\varepsilon > 0$), functional congruence
  is reflexive and symmetric but non-transitive, inducing a tolerance covering
  of overlapping neighborhoods rather than disjoint equivalence classes. The set
  $\{[G_R]\}$ in $H_s(S)$ is strictly defined at the $\varepsilon = 0$
  Audit-Mode quotient level.
- *Operational Alignment*: This quotient partition grounds the Behavioral
  Convergence Proxy (Path-Count Metric) directly in the topology, bounding
  Hartley entropy to finite, operationally distinguishable classes without
  demanding impossible metaphysical access to unobservable private nodes.

`[PROVISIONAL]`

## APPENDIX A — Architectural Model

This document serves as the structural authority and rejection filter for the
General Method for Refinement and Transmission of Ideas (GMRTI). Every
theoretical claim, operational protocol, or new concept introduced to the
framework must be mapped against these irreducible domain entities. If a new
addition cannot be expressed in terms of these entities, either the addition is
structural debt, or this model must be deliberately evolved.

### Aa — Irreducible Domain Entities
**Aa.1 Semantic Covenant**: The unique, accumulated configuration of relational
edges and conceptual regions held by a transmitting or receiving entity. It is
the fundamental bounded context of meaning.

**Aa.2 Topological Network**: The field-like structural model of a covenant.
Concepts are emergent densities in this field, not self-subsistent particles.

**Aa.3 Edge (and Edge Taxonomy)**: The primitive relational event from which all
structure is built (e.g., Inferential, Causal, Procedural, Normative). Meaning
resides in the edge, not the atom.

**Aa.4 Topological Depth**: The dependency hierarchy of the network, divided
into Surface Subgraphs, Structural Subgraphs, and Axiomatic Edge-Clusters.

**Aa.5 Semantic Entropy**: The log-measure of structural ambiguity in a
transmitted signal ($H_s(S) = \log_2 |\{[G_R]\}|$); the log-count of distinct
functional equivalence classes of receiving-covenant edge-configurations
compatible with the signal under the §8b.1 quotient.

**Aa.6 Divergence**: A topological or temporal failure in transmission between
covenants. Primary types include Anchor-Only Subgraph, Edge Divergence (and
Edge-Type Misattribution), and Temporal Drift.

**Aa.7 Comosí**: A functional, bounded overlap between covenants sufficient for
practical coordination, though not a guarantee of semantic identity.

**Aa.8 Polyphonic Encoding**: The deliberate provision of multiple structurally
independent edge-paths to the same semantic destination to combat semantic
entropy and redundancy-masking.

**Aa.9 Syntactic Edge-Forcing**: Constraining the receiving covenant's
interpretation by building mandatory edge-traversals directly into the
grammatical or compositional structure of the expression.

**Aa.10 Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)**: The retroactive, local
labeling of concept-types (§5a.4) and Tier 2 edge-types (§2a.5) deployed
specifically when Edge Divergence or Edge-Type Misattribution is detected.

**Aa.11 The Refinement Cycle (The Algorithm)**: The recursive
diagnostic-and-reconstruction sequence (Covenant Declaration -> Generative
Reconstruction -> Divergence Localization -> Targeted Repair -> Irreducibility).

**Aa.12 Constructo-Pragmatist Definition (CPD)**: The 7-tuple operational
specification framework $\langle P, \Sigma, T, I, E, Y, \Omega \rangle$
transforming refined semantic concepts into bounded, executable operational
architectures within declared domains.

**Aa.13 Allegorical Polyphony (Allegory)**: A concrete, domain-bounded
polyphonic-encoding path whose intermediate nodes and edges reside in an
accessible operational domain, preserving destination-isomorphism with an
abstract target concept to reduce transmission entropy.

## APPENDIX M — Methodology & Status Markers

This document defines the lifecycle, validation requirements, and epistemic
authority of theoretical claims within the GMRTI framework. It establishes the
non-negotiable behavioral contracts for authoring and refactoring the treatise.

### Ma — Status Markers and Invariant Graduation
**Ma.1** Theoretical claims within GMRTI are tagged with Status Markers. These
    markers dictate the claim's load-bearing authority and serve as invariants
    in the CLDS workflow:

- `[OPEN]`: A known unresolved design challenge. High semantic entropy is
  expected.
- `[PROVISIONAL]`: A claim held with operational confidence but still open to
  fundamental revision. It has not yet survived a full adversarial audit.
- `[STABLE]`: Authorial closure — a formal structural commitment not currently
  undergoing revision in order to prevent infinite regress and self-consuming
  refinement (see §3g.2). Changes here propagate broadly, so any refactor that
  alters a `[STABLE]` claim must deliberately invoke the Refinement Cycle.
  *Epistemic boundary*: `[STABLE]` marks authorial closure and dependency-clean
  integration (Ma.3), not an assertion of metaphysical truth, final proof, or
  permanent immunity from revision.
- `[RECURSIVE]`: A claim that applies to the framework itself and must be
  checked for self-consistency (e.g., the claim that all language is
  high-entropy applies to the language of GMRTI).
- `[ALLEGORY]` (or `[ALLEGORY: <TargetConcept>]`): A concrete domain-specific
  worked instantiation serving as an independent, destination-isomorphic
  polyphonic-encoding path to an abstract target concept.
- `[PLAIN]`: A plain-language seed sentence for a section or concept, written
  at a high-school reading level. Extracted by `pebble.py` to compile
  `PEBBLE.md`. Must appear as the first sub-paragraph of a section, before any
  formal notation or jargon is introduced. Never carries an epistemic claim —
  it is a transmission layer, not an invariant.

**Ma.2 — Compound Status Markers**

Claims may carry compound markers (e.g., `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`,
`[PROVISIONAL][ALLEGORY]`) when multiple orthogonal status dimensions intersect:

- **Primary Epistemic Dimension**: `[OPEN]`, `[PROVISIONAL]`, or `[STABLE]`,
  defining the claim's verification stage and invariant authority.
- **Secondary Reflexive / Structural Dimension**:
- `[RECURSIVE]`: Indicates that the claim reflexively binds the GMRTI's own
    authoring, representation, and transmission processes.
- `[ALLEGORY]`: Indicates a concrete polyphonic worked instantiation.
- `[PLAIN]`: Indicates a high-school-level entry ramp compiled into
    `PEBBLE.md`.


In compound forms, the primary epistemic status dictates the claim's invariant
rigidity; the secondary tag specifies its reflexive scope or structural role.

**Ma.3 — Invariant Dependency Constraint** `[STABLE]`

No `[STABLE]` claim or architectural invariant may rest upon, cite as
foundational, or require the truth of a `[PROVISIONAL]` or `[OPEN]` claim
without an explicit provisional dependency boundary declaration. When a
`[STABLE]` claim requires support from an evolving concept, the dependent
concept must either be audited and graduated to `[STABLE]` via the Refinement
Cycle, or the upstream claim must be constrained to a bounded heuristic scope.
Graduation is a structural, dependency-ordered operation: invariants may only
depend on validated invariants.

**Ma.4 — Graduation Criteria** `[STABLE]`

A claim graduates from `[PROVISIONAL]` to `[STABLE]` (authorial closure) when it
satisfies **Invariant Dependency (Ma.3)**: all concepts, mechanisms, and
definitions upon which the claim rests must themselves be `[STABLE]`, or carry
an explicit provisional dependency boundary declaration. A claim whose
load-bearing dependencies remain un-graduated without a declared boundary is
structurally blocked from `[STABLE]` status.

> [!NOTE]
> **Agentic Context vs. Provenance Tradeoff:** Extended audit logs and
> changelogs are valuable during philosophical development, and in an ideal
> world every claim would retain an explicit provenance and audit track record
> alongside it. In practice, carrying full audit history inside the treatise
> pollutes agentic context windows and degrades signal-to-noise. Because `git`
> already preserves the complete commit-level history implicitly, this
> repository deliberately trades in-document audit trails for a clean current
> monolith and periodically purges resolved logs via `nuke.ps1`.

## APPENDIX R — The Refinement Cycle

This document outlines the formalized workflow for evolving the framework. It is
the sanctioned operational pipeline for resolving structural gaps and addressing
semantic entropy within the GMRTI.

### Ra — The Resolution Pipeline (Dissonance -> Decisions -> REP)
The workflow for evolving the framework is a strict sequence:

- **Ra.1 Adversarial Audit (`DISSONANCE.md`)**: An auditor (human or agent)
  reviews the framework and logs structural gaps, contradictions, or high
  semantic entropy zones into a `DISSONANCE.md` artifact.
- **Ra.2 Context-Free Review**: Another context-free agent reviews the
  `DISSONANCE.md` against the current working revision of GMRTI to validate the
  claims.
- **Ra.3 Decision Log (`DECISIONS.md`)**: A `DECISIONS.md` file is generated,
  acting strictly as a bullet-point list of *WHAT* must be addressed (the
  marching orders).
- **Ra.4 Execution (The REP Cycle)**: The actual resolution of those decisions
  follows the Ratified Execution Protocol (see below).

### Rb — The Ratified Execution Protocol (REP) for Theoretical Authoring
The REP cycle guarantees that theoretical and structural integrity is
maintained. It must be strictly followed for all changes dictated by
`DECISIONS.md`:

- **Rb.1 Phase 1 (Minimal Plan Request)**: The AI proposes a skeletal outline of
  how to address a bullet point in `DECISIONS.md` (e.g., how to restructure a
  section). It lists new concepts and their architectural placement. No actual
  content is written.
- **Rb.2 Phase 2 (Ratification)**: You review the skeleton, apply architectural
  annotations, and reject or approve the approach.
- **Rb.3 Phase 3 (Full Plan Elaboration)**: The AI drafts `PLAN.md`, dividing
  the
  work into logical sequences with distinct boundaries.
- **Rb.4 Phase 4 (Sequential Execution)**: The AI updates the YAML specs and the
  Markdown source files sequentially. **Crucially**, this is an imperatively
  procedural execution: if something breaks on Sequence Phase 3, DO NOT PROCEED
  with Sequence Phase 4.
- **Rb.5 Phase 5 (Alignment Audit)**: A context-free review ensures the new
  content aligns with the ratified plan, verifies that no invariant was broken,
  and strictly validates the Invariant Dependency Constraint (Ma.3) and
  Graduation Criteria (Ma.4) before any `[PROVISIONAL]` → `[STABLE]` graduation
  is ratified.

### Rc — Tooling Verification & Execution Boundaries
All REP execution boundaries must be verified locally before transition to the
next phase:

- **Rc.1 Formatting Check**: `python wrap.py --check` ensures all markdown and
  YAML sources adhere to the canonical line width without untracked drift.
- **Rc.2 Monolithic Synchronization**: `python rewrite.py --check` guarantees
  the
  root monolithic document is in strict byte-level sync with modular sources.
- **Rc.3 Specification & Unit Tests**: `python -m pytest tests/ -v` validates
  spec schemas, link integrity, and parser contracts.

Per `AGENTS.md`, verification is performed exclusively locally via these Python
scripts and `pytest`.

justifying an operational apparatus expires, the apparatus itself is dissolved
rather than persisting indefinitely. `[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Semantic Covenant
(*convenio semántico*) — The totality of meaning-relations held by a rational
entity at a given moment; a personal, circumstantial, and mutable directed
graph. Not a fixed dictionary but a living topology. `[STABLE]`

#### * Comosí
The functional overlap between two [[Semantic Covenant|semantic covenants]]
sufficient to produce apparent understanding within a bounded domain, without
guaranteeing identity of underlying edge-structure. `[STABLE]`

#### * Apodictic Comosí
A functional overlap between [[Semantic Covenant|covenants]] so tightly
constrained by [[Syntactic Edge-Forcing]] (e.g., formal mathematical notation)
that residual [[Semantic Entropy]] is negligible relative to natural language
within a trained community. `[STABLE]`

#### * Metaagnosticism
The epistemological position that: (a) precise definitions are structurally
unavailable due to the circular, self-referential nature of
language-as-symbol-system; and (b) the impossibility of verifying whether
certainty has been achieved is itself a first-order epistemic condition, not
merely a practical limitation. The *meta* in metaagnosticism names precisely
this second clause: not only is certainty unavailable, but the verification of
whether one has achieved it is also unavailable — making the epistemic limit
operate at one level above ordinary fallibilism. `[STABLE][RECURSIVE]`

#### * Semantic Entropy
The log-measure of structural ambiguity in a transmitted signal, denoted as
$H_s(S)$. Represents the log-count of distinct functional equivalence classes of
receiving-covenant edge-configurations compatible with the signal under the
[[Graph-Space Partitioning Axiom]] (a class $[G_R]$ is compatible with $S$ iff
at least one representative $G \in [G_R]$ satisfies $S$). In practice, it is
operationalized via the [[Behavioral Convergence Proxy]] (Path-Count Metric).
`[STABLE]` [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3, §8b.1]

#### * Behavioral Convergence Proxy
The operational path-count metric for [[Semantic Entropy]]: the number of
independent [[Polyphonic Encoding]] paths that must be successfully
reconstructed and verified by the receiver under Audit-Mode
[[Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)|DRC]] before reaching the
[[Operational Action Threshold]]. `[STABLE]` [provisional dependency boundary:
§3c.3, §8b.1]

#### * Graph-Space Partitioning Axiom
The formal individuation criterion resolving §8b.1: under Audit-Mode exact
equivalence ($\varepsilon = 0$ on a declared finite operational probe suite),
functional equivalence is transitive and discretizes candidate
receiving-covenant graph space into a genuine quotient partition of equivalence
classes $\{[G_R]\}$ (two topologies belong to the same class if and only if they
license indistinguishable downstream operational inferences across all declared
probes). Under tolerance-bounded [[Comosí|comosí]] ($\varepsilon > 0$),
functional congruence is reflexive and symmetric but non-transitive, inducing a
tolerance covering rather than disjoint equivalence classes. `[PROVISIONAL]`
[provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3]

#### * Transmission
The attempted transfer of semantic content from one
[[Semantic Covenant|covenant]] to another, or from one temporal state of a
covenant to a later state of the same covenant. `[STABLE]`

#### * Refinement
The iterative process of improving the internal coherence and transmissibility
of a framework within and across [[Semantic Covenant|semantic covenants]].
`[STABLE]`

#### * Divergence
Any detectable mismatch between two [[Semantic Covenant|covenants']] handling of
the same conceptual region. `[STABLE]`

#### * Surface Subgraph
An edge-cluster in a [[Semantic Covenant]] that is highly context-dependent,
easily renegotiated, and minimally load-bearing; repairable through single-path
clarification (§2b). `[STABLE]`

#### * Structural Subgraph
An edge-cluster that routes meaning between large regions of a
[[Semantic Covenant]]; revision produces downstream effects on dependent
[[Surface Subgraph|Surface Subgraphs]] (§2b). `[STABLE]`

#### * Axiomatic Edge-Cluster
An edge-cluster with maximal structural centrality within a
[[Semantic Covenant]], flagged as *Axiomatically Active* by the
[[Dependency-Tracing Scan]]; alteration requires rebuilding the covenant's
identity (§2b). `[STABLE]`

#### * Anchor-Only Subgraph
A divergence type in which the receiver holds a shared surface label with zero
internal relational depth attached to it, requiring [[Polyphonic Encoding]] to
construct internal topology (§2c.1i). `[STABLE]`

#### * Temporal Drift Divergence
Divergence arising not from difference between two people, but from a single
[[Semantic Covenant]] changing over time between encoding ($t_1$) and decoding
($t_2$), addressed by the [[Temporal Stranger]] protocol (§2c.1iii). `[STABLE]`

#### * Federated Graph
The distributed, externalized semantic structure of an institution or community,
stabilized via [[Artifact Anchor|Artifact Anchors]] rather than collective
consciousness. `[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Artifact Anchor
An external, relatively immutable scaffold (written law, canonical text,
standardized protocol) that forces constituent individual
[[Semantic Covenant|covenants]] to continuously realign their
[[Structural Subgraph|Structural Subgraphs]] to a shared artificial topology.
`[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)
A context-sensitive parameter defined on the bounded interval $[0.0, 1.0]$
governing the required precision of reconstruction; inversely proportional to
interpretive charity. Calibrating DRC toward $0.0$ maximizes interpretive
charity (Generative Mode); calibrating DRC toward $1.0$ drives charity toward
zero and enforces strict verification (Audit Mode via [[The Mirror]]). The
assignment of numerical DRC values to specific domains is deliberately
un-prescribed by [[GMRTI]], as risk ranking requires normative value judgments
that belong exclusively to the participating covenants. `[STABLE]`

#### * Meta-DRC Divergence
A disagreement between communicating covenants regarding the
[[Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)]] classification of a domain, which pauses
primary [[Transmission|transmission]] and defaults to the highest demanded DRC
of any participating party (given a declared commensurate stake) until resolved.
`[STABLE]`

#### * Rational Agnosticism
The operational posture of deliberate suspension in which a receiver treats
themselves as possessing only the edges explicitly declared in the current
transmission, surfacing hidden contextual assumptions (§2e). `[STABLE]`

#### * Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)
The practice of deploying ontological type distinctions retroactively and
locally, only at the precise site of a detected [[Divergence|Edge Divergence]],
rather than as upfront metalanguage. `[STABLE]`

#### * Polyphonic Encoding
The deliberate provision of multiple structurally independent edge-paths to the
same semantic destination within a single [[Transmission|transmission]] act. The
primary proactive transmission fidelity strategy. `[STABLE]`

#### * Allegory (Worked Instantiation)
A concrete, domain-bounded [[Polyphonic Encoding|polyphonic-encoding]] path
whose intermediate nodes and edges reside in an accessible operational domain,
preserving destination-isomorphism with an abstract target concept
($\operatorname{Action}_C(A) \cong
\operatorname{Action}_C(\operatorname{Allegory}(A))$) under
[[Core Path Minimization]] (§2f.6) to reduce [[Semantic Entropy]]. `[STABLE]`
[provisional dependency boundary: §7d.1]

#### * Redundancy-Masking
The failure mode in which successful [[Polyphonic Encoding]] conceals a local
[[Anchor-Only Subgraph]] that would produce transmission failure under
single-path conditions. `[STABLE]`

#### * Path-Stripping Probing
The diagnostic protocol for detecting [[Redundancy-Masking]] by temporarily
disabling all but one encoding path to check if the receiver's reconstruction
still satisfies the [[Operational Action Threshold]]. `[STABLE]` [provisional
dependency boundary: §3c.3]

#### * Proactive Chaotic Probing (PCP)
The scheduled maintenance protocol that runs [[Path-Stripping Probing]] on
random samples of polyphonically encoded nodes and candidate disjunctive hubs
during audit states to surface latent divergence before real-time faults occur
(§2f.5v). `[STABLE]`

#### * Syntactic Edge-Forcing
A transmission fidelity mechanism in which the grammatical or compositional
structure of an expression compels the receiver to traverse a specific
relational path through semantic space in order to decode the expression. In the
v0.9 prototype, this is implemented by mandating canonical relational operators
(e.g. `[->causal]`, `[->inferential]`) in key structural claims. `[STABLE]`

#### * Edge Taxonomy
The partitioned classification of relation-types. Operates as a two-tiered
system: Tier 1 (Causal/Inferential/Other) for initial [[Covenant Declaration]],
and Tier 2 (8-type taxonomy) deployed locally via
[[Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)|JIT-OM]] for
[[Edge-Type Misattribution]] repair. `[STABLE]`

#### * Edge-Type Misattribution
A subtype of [[Divergence|Edge Divergence]] in which both covenants possess the
A-to-B relation but assign it to different edge-types in the [[Edge Taxonomy]] —
one treating it as inferential, the other as causal; one as normative, the other
as affective. Distinct from topological divergence within a shared edge-type,
and requiring a different repair strategy. `[STABLE]`

#### * Bad Actor
An adversarial participant who exploits interpretive charity by declaring
internally contradictory edge subgraphs, manufacturing spurious irreducibility,
demanding unbacked `DRC = 1.0`, or rejecting repairs verified by the
[[Topological Consistency Audit]] without supplying a distinguishing boundary
condition (§3g.4). `[STABLE]`

#### * Topological Consistency Audit
A diagnostic protocol to detect [[Bad Actor|Bad Actors]] by auditing the logical
consistency of their declared edge subgraphs and flagging contradictions or
bad-faith rejection of isomorphic repairs. `[STABLE]`

#### * Periodic Re-Mirroring Protocol
A methodological check to combat [[Artifact Anchor]] decay by periodically
evaluating the [[Federated Graph]] against the Anchor under [[The Mirror]]
posture to produce a desynchronization Delta Graph. `[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Dependency-Tracing Scan
A scan of the topological network that calculates the downstream propagation
impact of altering specific edge-clusters, flagging those above a configurable
node threshold (default 30%, subject to empirical calibration) as
[[Axiomatic Edge-Cluster|Axiomatically Active]]. `[STABLE]`

#### * Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation
A formal containment wrapper drawn around a repaired subgraph that treats it as
a black-box node with declared interfaces, attenuating (not eliminating)
localized meaning-shifts from propagating holistically across the network.
Provides practical isolation bounded by interface edge count; residual holistic
leakage through interface-edge context change is a known structural limitation
(see 2h.3). `[STABLE]`

#### * Covenant Zero Protocol (CZP)
The ostensive association procedure that bootstraps the [[Primitive Covenant]]
(`causal`, `inferential`, `identity`) from the framework-independent
[[Minimal Substrate]] (§2e.5iii). `[STABLE]`

#### * Primitive Covenant
The GMRTI-specific shared substrate consisting of the three core primitive
relational operators (`[->causal]`, `[->inferential]`, `[->identity]`)
bootstrapped via [[Covenant Zero Protocol (CZP)|CZP]], with `[->procedural]`,
`[->referential]`, and `[->structural]` derived compositionally and
`[->normative]` and `[->affective]` negotiated via
[[Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)|JIT-OM]] (see §2e.5ii), together
with the edge-primacy structural commitment and the Tier 1 coarse taxonomy.
Required for GMRTI-mediated communication specifically; sits above the
framework-independent [[Minimal Substrate]] (2e.5i). Its establishment between
agents is itself a transmission problem. `[STABLE]`

#### * Minimal Substrate
The weakest framework-independent assumption for structured communication:
agents can distinguish signals from non-signals, detect structural similarity,
and individuate their own conceptual regions. Carries no GMRTI-specific content.
`[STABLE]`

#### * Minimal Intelligibility Bootstrap
The stratified decoding substrate: the [[Minimal Substrate]]
(framework-independent) guarantees basic signal decoding; the
[[Primitive Covenant]] (GMRTI-specific) enables GMRTI-mediated repair.
Calibrating [[Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)|DRC]] to maximum (1.0) reduces
charity to the Minimal Substrate baseline. `[STABLE]`

#### * Operational Action Threshold
The domain-specific threshold of reconstruction fidelity at which a receiver's
reconstructed semantic clustering produces downstream operational choices,
inferences, or behaviors that are functionally congruent with the transmission
across all declared boundary conditions. Serves as the termination criterion for
[[Operational Parking]] (§3a.1i) and [[Path-Stripping Probing]] (§2f.5iv).
`[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Core Path Minimization
The complexity governor requiring that redundant paths in
[[Polyphonic Encoding]] maintain destination-isomorphism (terminating at the
identical semantic destination and licensing the same downstream inferences)
while preserving route-independence across disjoint intermediate topological
structures. `[STABLE]`

#### * Dynamic Re-Anchoring
The process of treating [[Artifact Anchor|Artifact Anchors]] as an exponentially
smoothed moving average, updated via continuous correction vectors to bound
tracking lag against textual drift (§4b.3). `[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * The Mirror
Any [[Rational Agnosticism|Rationally Agnostic]] receiver (human, LLM, or
future-self) operationalized to reconstruct a transmission using strictly
declared edges, with interpretive charity calibrated to the
[[Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)]]. A tool within the [[GMRTI]] protocol, not the
protocol itself. `[STABLE]`

#### * Temporal Stranger
The past self treated as a Foreign Mind: the primary operationalization of
Self-to-Future-Self [[Transmission|transmission]] repair. `[STABLE]`

#### * Covenant Declaration
Stage 1 of the GMRTI algorithm: exposing the transmitting covenant's key
topological regions, edges, and Tier 1 coarse cuts so divergence can be detected
(§3b). `[STABLE]`

#### * Generative Reconstruction
Stage 2 of the GMRTI algorithm: Mirror mapping followed by DRC-calibrated
interpretive bridging, producing a candidate reconstruction and divergence
report (§3c). `[STABLE]`

#### * Divergence Localization
Stage 3 of the GMRTI algorithm: tracing detected divergence from
[[Surface Subgraph|Surface]] to [[Structural Subgraph|Structural]] to
[[Axiomatic Edge-Cluster|Axiomatic]] depth layers (§3d). `[STABLE]`

#### * Targeted Repair
Stage 4 of the GMRTI algorithm: applying Scaffolding, Local Alignment with
[[Boundary-Tracing Encapsulation]],
[[Just-In-Time Ontological Marking (JIT-OM)|JIT-OM]] type declaration, or
Archival Reconstruction (§3e). `[STABLE]`

#### * Operational Parking
Algorithmic termination when reconstructed [[Comosí|comosí]] meets the
[[Operational Action Threshold]] for the bounded domain (§3a.1i). `[STABLE]`

#### * Cost-Bounded Termination
Algorithmic termination when the expected epistemic gain of further refinement
falls below the [[Domain Risk Coefficient (DRC)|DRC]]-adjusted cost (§3a.1iii,
§3g). `[STABLE]`

#### * Irreducibility Declaration
Stage 5 of the GMRTI algorithm: provisional formal acknowledgment that a
divergence at the [[Axiomatic Edge-Cluster|Axiomatic]] layer reflects a genuine
difference in foundational commitments rather than transmission failure, handing
off to [[GMCR]] (§3f). `[STABLE]`

#### * Epistemic Termination
Termination of the GMRTI cycle via an [[Irreducibility Declaration]] at Stage 5
(§3a.1ii, §3f). `[STABLE]`

#### * Self-Annotation
Applying GMRTI's Covenant Declaration, edge-typing, and temporal-anchoring
checks to one's own notes and artifacts as a single practitioner — the baseline
sandbox for Self-to-Future-Self transmission (§1c.1). `[STABLE]`

#### * Maintenance Covenant
A prescriptive interpretive body paired with an [[Artifact Anchor]] to
continuously audit, negotiate, and counteract semantic drift via
[[Periodic Re-Mirroring Protocol|Periodic Re-Mirroring]] (§2d.2).
`[PROVISIONAL]`

#### * Null-Effect Equivalence Axiom
The provisional rule that latent topological divergence with no behavioral
consequence across all declared boundary conditions of the active domain is
treated as operationally null under [[Comosí|comosí]] until boundary conditions
expand (§2f.5v). `[STABLE]` [provisional dependency boundary: §3c.3]
